Our project will translate research outputs in gendered mobility analytics into products for transit agencies: a multilingual, culturally sensitive AI platform that detects, monitors, and explains gendered mobility risks in metro systems. The product integrates three assets developed in this project: (i) a domain‑adapted Large language model (LLM) for gendered sentiment and incident context understanding, (ii) a robust data pipeline for ingesting and anonymizing social media and focus group insights, and (iii) an interactive dashboard that displays spatio‑temporal hotspots, trends, and intervention impacts. The platform’s value proposition is to provide operators and policymakers with real‑time intelligence to reduce harassment, improve perceived safety, and target investments with measurable returns, thereby increasing women’s transit participation and supporting broader equity.

We will first mature the prototype dashboard into a deployable product by developing the data ingestion pipeline, improving the LLM’s multilingual performance and implementing station‑level geospatial analytics with anomaly detection. We will also modularize the architecture: a back‑end API layer (data services, model services, authorization), a privacy‑preserving data lake with audit logs, and a front‑end web client for role‑based views (operations room, safety team, planning, and executive summary). Post‑GRF, we will pursue Innovation and Technology Fund (ITF) support to deliver enterprise‑grade features and operator‑facing training packages.

Deployment will accommodate both SaaS and models to meet data sensitivity and compliance requirements. For Hong Kong and many Asian operators, privacy and reputational risks necessitate private network deployments; therefore, we will provide a containerized on‑prem option with offline model inference and batch updates. Both models expose secure APIs for data push/pull, enabling integration with incident reporting, CCTV analytics, staff rostering, and existing GIS systems. The LLM service will be deployed as a microservice with policy‑based prompt and output filtering to prevent sensitive leakage.

Data governance and ethics are foundational. We will implement a privacy‑by‑design framework, including data minimization, differential privacy where appropriate, and systematic removal of direct identifiers. Social media data will be processed under local laws with strong bot filtering and anonymization. Both social media and focus group data will be consented, de‑identified, and stored separately with strict access control. Model governance will include bias assessment across languages and demographics and human‑in‑the‑loop review protocols for high‑impact insights. We will provide clear data processing agreements and retention schedules to institutional partners.
