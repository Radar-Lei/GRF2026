# Education Plan

The proposed project will provide students with a unique opportunity to develop advanced skills in social media analytics, artificial intelligence, and transport equity research while addressing critical gendered mobility barriers in metro systems. These skills are highly relevant to the contemporary challenges of creating inclusive urban transportation and developing ethical AI applications for social good, and students will gain valuable, practical experience through their involvement in cutting-edge research that combines big data analytics with qualitative research methods.

Students participating in this project will acquire expertise in utilizing state-of-the-art tools for analyzing social media data and developing customized large language models. A key benefit will be exposure to the integration of large-scale digital data (social media content) with targeted qualitative data (focus group insights) to understand how gendered experiences shape women's mobility patterns and access to urban opportunities. Through working with both quantitative and qualitative data streams, students will develop a comprehensive understanding of mixed-methods research design and bias correction frameworks essential for responsible AI development in social science applications.

As part of the research process, students will assist in multilingual social media data collection, demographic inference using advanced machine learning techniques, and the development of domain-specific sentiment analysis models. This hands-on experience will provide students with real-world understanding of how AI-driven transport research can inform policy and design solutions for creating more equitable metro systems. Additionally, participating in LLM-assisted focus groups across diverse cultural contexts will help students better understand cross-cultural research methodologies and the importance of addressing representativeness limitations in digital data sources.

The research team will integrate project outcomes into courses offered at the university, such as urban planning, gender studies, and data science programs. For example, the global dataset of gendered metro experiences developed through Task 1 and Task 2 will be used in courses like GRMD2501 Theory and Practice of Smart Cities, taught by the PI, to allow students to apply their learning to actual case studies of transport equity. Furthermore, the development of customized large language models and interactive dashboards will be incorporated into courses focusing on AI ethics, smart cities, and sustainable urban development, ensuring that students can apply cutting-edge AI tools to real-world social challenges.

The project is particularly relevant to students in the department's interdisciplinary programs that combine technology with social impact research. The project findings will support the development of teaching materials that allow students to engage with the latest methods in social media analytics, natural language processing, and ethical AI development. These materials will encourage students to explore AI's potential for addressing gender inequality and transport barriers while maintaining cultural sensitivity and ethical standards. By applying theoretical knowledge to practical applications that directly impact women's mobility and urban access, students will be better prepared to contribute to the future of inclusive urban planning and responsible AI development.

Students will have the chance to contribute to policy recommendations and dashboard development that translate research findings into actionable tools for metro operators and urban planners. This involvement will give them direct experience in creating evidence-based visualization platforms that support data-driven decision making for transport equity interventions. The students' engagement in this collaborative process will foster an understanding of how to bridge academic research with practical policy implementation, particularly in addressing spatial and temporal patterns of gendered mobility concerns across different cultural and economic contexts.

In sum, the project's educational activities will provide students with a broad range of skills, from advanced social media analytics and large language model development to transport equity research and cross-cultural policy analysis. The interdisciplinary nature of this project will enrich the learning experience, equipping students with both technical expertise and social awareness to develop AI-driven solutions that promote gender equality and inclusive urban mobility in an increasingly connected global society.
