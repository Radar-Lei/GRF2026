The proposed project will provide students with a unique opportunity to develop advanced skills in urban planning, street view data analysis, and generative AI applications for designing age-friendly neighborhoods. These skills are highly relevant to the contemporary challenge of creating livable environments for an aging population, and students will gain valuable, practical experience through their involvement in the project activities.

Students participating in this project will acquire expertise in utilizing cutting-edge tools for analyzing the built environment. A key benefit will be exposure to the integration of big data (i.e., street view images) with small data (i.e., survey responses) to understand how physical neighborhood features influence older adults’ activities and well-being. Through working with both data streams, students will develop a comprehensive understanding of how urban environments can be modified to meet the needs of older adults.

As part of the research process, students will assist in data collection, including street view image analysis, recruiting older adults for surveys, and processing open-ended survey responses. This hands-on experience will provide students with a real-world understanding of how data-driven urban planning can inform policy and design solutions. Additionally, participating in workshops at senior centers will help students to better understand older people's perspectives on their neighborhood environments and contextualize the data they analyze.

The research team will integrate project outcomes into courses offered at the university, such as urban planning, geography, and data science programs. For example, the dataset developed from the street view analysis (Task 1) will be used in courses like GRMD2501 Theory and Practice of Smart Cities, taught by the PI, to allow students to apply their learning to actual case studies. Furthermore, the development of generative AI tools for urban design will be incorporated into courses focusing on smart cities and sustainable urban development. This integration will ensure that students can apply AI-driven tools to real-world urban challenges.

The project is particularly relevant to students in the department’s Area of Concentration– Smart Sustainable Cities, of which the PI serves as the coordinator. The project findings will support the development of teaching materials that will allow students to engage with the latest methods in machine learning and urban design. These materials will encourage students to explore AI’s potential for creating more inclusive, age-friendly urban spaces. By applying theoretical knowledge to practical applications, students will be better prepared to contribute to the future of urban planning in aging societies.

Finally, students will have the chance to contribute to policy recommendations and urban design solutions (Task 4). This involvement will give them direct experience in translating research findings into actionable insights for urban planners, government agencies, and community organizations. The students’ engagement in this collaborative process will foster an understanding of how to create evidence-based design solutions that promote active aging.

In sum, the project’s educational activities will provide students with a broad range of skills, from big data analytics and the use of generative AI to urban planning and policy development. The interdisciplinary nature of this project will enrich the learning experience, equipping students with the tools and knowledge to develop age-friendly urban environments.
