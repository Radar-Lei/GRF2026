Title: Barriers to Women’s Mobility in Metro Transit: Uncovering Gendered Experiences through Social Media Analytics

# Abstract

Urbanization has significantly improved transport infrastructure worldwide, yet metro systems do not serve all population groups equally. Women face distinct mobility challenges including voyeurism, sexual harassment, and disrespect for breastfeeding—issues that remain largely invisible due to social norms, cultural pressures, and fear of stigma. This problem is particularly acute in developing countries where limited surveillance exacerbates women's vulnerability.

Traditional research methods such as questionnaire surveys, interviews, and focus groups have provided valuable qualitative insights into women's mobility experiences, but they face significant limitations in scale, real-time data capture, and the ability to uncover unspoken realities across diverse cultural contexts. These methods often suffer from sampling biases, social desirability bias, and limited geographical coverage, particularly in developing countries where official reporting mechanisms are inadequate. Moreover, traditional approaches struggle to capture the spontaneous, real-time nature of gendered experiences that often go unreported due to stigma and cultural pressures.

This research employs an innovative social media analytics approach to overcome these limitations and capture and analyze unspoken, real-world experiences of women in metro systems globally. We propose a mixed-method framework that: (1) leverages multilingual social media data mining to uncover gendered experiences across diverse contexts; (2) develops customized large language models for accurate sentiment analysis and nuanced interpretation of gendered mobility barriers; and (3) integrates digital analytics with group focus study in selected Asian cities.

Our approach advances transport equity research through three key innovations: harnessing social media analytics to reveal underreported realities, building culturally sensitive language models for multilingual analysis, and establishing methodological triangulation through mixed-methods design. The study will generate a unique global dataset of gendered metro experiences, provide actionable insights for policymakers and transit operators, and contribute to creating safer, more equitable urban environments that empower women's mobility and access to opportunities.

# 1. Objectives and Pathways to Impact Statement a) Project Objectives (max. 800 words)

Urbanization has significantly improved transport infrastructure and mobility in cities worldwide, with an increasing number of cities operating or planning metro systems. However, advanced public transport systems like metros do not serve all population groups equally. The transport equity literature has shown that different social strata benefit unevenly from transit services. Women, in particular, often face distinct mobility challenges compared with men. Many of these challenges—such as voyeurism, sexual harassment, and the disrespect of breastfeeding—are underreported due to social norms, cultural pressures, and fear of stigma. As a result, they remain largely invisible to passengers, operators, and policymakers, and receive limited coverage in mainstream media. This issue is particularly acute in developing countries, where limited surveillance and policing exacerbate the problem. To address this gap, the proposed research will use a social media analytics approach to capture and analyse unspoken, real-world experiences of women in metro systems globally. Theoretically, it contributes to the transport equity discourse by foregrounding gendered mobility barriers. Methodologically, it introduces a novel application of social media analytics in transport research. Empirically, it will build a unique dataset of gendered metro experiences across diverse contexts. Practically, the study will provide actionable insights for policymakers and transit operators to improve safety, inclusivity, and equity in metro systems.

Achieving transport equity requires addressing the needs of socially disadvantaged groups, including women. A safer and more inclusive travel experience can increase women’s mobility, empowering them to access employment, education, and social opportunities across the city and beyond. From a societal perspective, improving women’s experiences in public transit not only enhances their well-being but also supports broader goals of social inclusion and gender equality. In advanced economies such as Japan and Hong Kong, urban metros are the backbone of the transport network, carrying the majority of daily trips. Yet even in these systems, women often feel unsafe, particularly at night or in sparsely populated areas and lines. In contrast, in cities where metros are less dominant and public transport use is lower, reduced crowds do not necessarily translate into safety. Limited public surveillance can increase women’s vulnerability to harassment and other harmful acts. By systematically addressing these challenges, this research will advance knowledge on gender and mobility, inform evidencebased interventions, and ultimately contribute to creating safer, more equitable urban environments that benefit society as a whole.

## Objective 1: To select a list of representative cities and to collect data on passengers' perception of metro systems

For city selection for case studies, we will employ a comprehensive set of indicators that capture economic development, gender equality, urban characteristics, and digital infrastructure. Beyond GDP per capita and the Gender Inequality Index (GII), our selection framework includes: (1) internet penetration rates to evaluate digital accessibility; (2) urban development indicators including population density and service sector employment; (3) transport-specific metrics such as metro usage patterns; and (5) additional gender-specific indicators including educational attainment gaps and labor force participation differentials.

The rationale for this multidimensional approach is threefold. First, it ensures representation across diverse economic and social contexts. Second, the inclusion of digital infrastructure indicators is crucial for our social media analytics methodology, as countries with higher internet penetration are more likely to generate substantial social media content relevant to metro experiences. Third, by incorporating urban and transport-specific metrics, we capture contextual factors that influence metro system operations and passenger experiences. Preliminary analysis (see Appendix) demonstrates the distribution of countries with metro systems across GDP and GII quadrants, revealing opportunities for comparative analysis across different development levels and gender equality contexts.

## Objective 2: To develop user profiling techniques for identifying gendered experiences and extracting authentic metro-related social media data

The second objective focuses on developing advanced user profiling and demographic inference techniques to accurately identify female metro users and extract authentic gendered travel experiences from social media data. Building on state-of-the-art demographic inference methods, we will create a multi-modal framework that integrates name-based matching, linguistic analysis, social network propagation, and profile image analysis to infer user gender and demographic attributes across diverse cultural contexts.

Our approach will leverage multiple user signals including: (1) name-based matching using culturally-specific name dictionaries to infer gender and ethnicity; (2) linguistic analysis of user posts and self-descriptions to identify gender-associated language patterns; (3) social network analysis using graph propagation techniques (Label Propagation and Graph Convolutional Networks) to infer demographic attributes based on homophily patterns; and (4) profile image analysis using computer vision techniques to extract gender-related visual features. This multi-modal framework will enable robust gender identification even in low-resource scenarios where traditional supervised learning approaches face data scarcity challenges.

## Objective 3: To develop a large language model for sentiment analysis of gendered experiences on the metro systems

The third objective focuses on developing a customized large language model (LLM) specifically designed for sentiment analysis of gendered experiences in metro systems. We will create a specialized LLM architecture that integrates multilingual processing capabilities with domain-specific knowledge about transit operations and gendered mobility patterns. This model will be trained on carefully curated social media data from the selected cities, incorporating both textual content and associated spatio-temporal metadata to enable context-aware sentiment classification.

Our approach will leverage state-of-the-art sentiment models (such as RoBERTa) fine-tuned with transit-specific vocabulary and gendered experience indicators. The model will be designed to recognize expressions of safety concerns, sexual harassment experiences, comfort levels, and service quality perceptions that are often expressed indirectly or through culturally specific linguistic patterns. By incorporating spatio-temporal encoders, the model will learn to associate sentiments with specific operational contexts (e.g., peak hours, particular stations or lines), enabling more accurate interpretation of gendered experiences across diverse cultural and linguistic contexts. This customized LLM will overcome the limitations of generic sentiment analysis tools by providing domain-adapted, culturally sensitive, and contextually aware analysis of women's metro experiences globally.

## Objective 4: To develop an integrated dashboard for visualizing gendered metro experiences and triangulate findings through focus group studies

The fourth objective combines the development of an interactive visualization dashboard with comprehensive focus group studies to create a holistic understanding of gendered metro experiences. Building upon the outputs from Objectives 2 and 3, we will develop an integrated platform that visualizes spatio-temporal patterns of gendered service quality perceptions while incorporating qualitative insights from direct engagement with women metro users.

The dashboard component will feature interactive capabilities for visualizing sentiment scores, geographic locations of metro stations and lines, temporal patterns (hourly, daily, seasonal variations), and gender-specific sentiment distributions. It will employ heatmaps to display spatial concentrations of gendered safety concerns, time-series charts to reveal temporal patterns, and comparative visualizations to highlight gender disparities across different operational contexts. Policymakers and transit operators will be able to filter data by specific time periods, metro lines, stations, and service quality dimensions to identify hotspots and patterns of gendered mobility barriers.

The focus group component will employ a mixed-methods approach to validate and contextualize insights from social media analytics. Through structured discussions in three selected Asian cities, we will triangulate quantitative findings with rich qualitative data, exploring how women articulate their perceptions of metro experiences, how these perceptions are interpreted in specific cultural contexts, and how they relate to actual travel behaviors. This integrated approach ensures that digital insights accurately reflect lived experiences while revealing the consequences of perceived barriers on women's access to urban opportunities.

Note: We can use the case of Hong Kong to demonstrate the dashboard, and research collaborators will help conduct focus group studies in the selected cities.

# i. Research Context

## Gendered Mobility Barriers in Metro Systems

Women face distinct mobility challenges in public transit systems worldwide, with research consistently demonstrating pervasive yet underreported gendered barriers due to social norms and fear of stigma (Mazumder & Pokharel, 2018; King et al., 2021). These challenges encompass profound safety concerns, with women reporting significantly higher levels of fear and insecurity compared to men in metro environments (Ouali et al., 2020; Lindkvist, 2024).Historical and socio-cultural factors deeply influence women's mobility patterns. Becker's (2019) work reveals how historical norms continue to shape contemporary mobility restrictions, while Field and Vyborny (2022) demonstrate that safety and social acceptability constitute primary barriers to women's mobility, highlighting the importance of gender-sensitive transport services.

The manifestations of gendered barriers vary across contexts but share common themes of harassment and restricted access. From sexual violence in Bangladesh (Mazumder & Pokharel, 2018) to heightened insecurity in Latin America (Infante-Vargas & Boyer, 2021; Busco et al., 2022), women become "captive travelers" forced to prioritize safety over affordability through extensive behavioral adaptations.Beyond safety concerns, women face significant time poverty exacerbated by entrenched gender roles and caregiving responsibilities (Zehba M. P. et al., 2025). This time poverty, combined with the emotional "affective ordeals" of unsafe conditions (Roy et al., 2024), creates complex mobility constraints of women that traditional research methods often fail to capture comprehensively.

A systematic review of the literature reveals a critical scarcity of comprehensive questionnaire surveys and focus group studies specifically addressing women's mobility barriers in metro transit systems. Globally, only a handful of surveys have been conducted, with most studies employing small sample sizes (typically 8-38 participants for qualitative interviews and focus groups) and limited geographical coverage. We identified only 3 major questionnaire surveys with sample sizes exceeding 500 respondents across the entire Asian continent: a study in Hyderabad, India (583 women commuters), research across eight European countries (1406 female travelers), and limited surveys in cities in China, which represents a severe underrepresentation regarding the research topic. The methodological landscape is dominated by small-scale qualitative approaches: Kerzhner et al. (2018) used only 8 interviews and 38 focus group participants in Jerusalem, Kacharo et al. (2022) employed 24 focus group members in Hawassa, Ethiopia, Infante-Vargas & Boyer (2021) conducted only 11 interviews in Saltillo, Mexico, and Sil et al. (2023) highlighted the prevalence of small-sample studies across South and Southeast Asia. Roy et al. (2024) employed visual surveys in Kolkata, and most studies rely on samples of 24-38 focus group participants. Quantitative surveys with robust statistical power are exceptionally rare, particularly those employing mixed-methods designs that could capture both the scale and nuance of gendered mobility barriers. This research vacuum is particularly acute in developing Asian countries where cultural norms and fear of stigma further suppress reporting of sensitive issues like sexual harassment and safety concerns.

## Methodological Innovations in Social Media Analytics

The limitations of traditional methods in capturing underreported gendered experiences have prompted innovative approaches using social media analytics (SMA). SMA offers real-time insights into public perceptions, complementing traditional survey and qualitative methods (Thelwall, 2017; Sufi, 2023). In transport research, SMA has demonstrated significant potential for uncovering service quality issues. Luo and He (2020, 2021) pioneered social media mining for transit services, revealing temporal and spatial variations in perceptions while identifying distinct gender gaps in concerns—with women focusing more on comfort and safety. The methodological landscape is rapidly evolving with advanced computational techniques. Deep learning approaches (Hayat et al., 2019) and transformer models like DistilBERT (Alharbi & Haq, 2024) have enhanced processing power and predictive accuracy for complex text analysis tasks in transportation contexts. The emergence of Large Language Models represents a transformative advancement. Specialized LLMs fine-tuned on domain-specific data demonstrate potential for understanding nuanced expressions in specific contexts, with multimodal capabilities enhancing analysis of visual and textual information interplay (Radwan et al., 2024; Y. Li et al., 2023).

## Addressing the User Profiling

A critical methodological challenge in gendered mobility research is accurate demographic identification, particularly gender attribution from social media data. Traditional approaches relying on self-reported gender or platform data face limitations from privacy concerns, incomplete profiles, and cultural variations in gender expression, leading to a fundamental barrier to distinguishing authentic gendered experiences from general transit feedback (Liu et al., 2023). Recent advances in multi-modal demographic inference offer promising solutions. Techniques combining name-based prediction, linguistic style analysis, social network propagation, and profile image analysis demonstrate improved accuracy in cross-cultural gender attribution (Wang & Zhang, 2022). The integration of advanced user profiling with sentiment analysis represents a novel contribution to transport equity research. By developing robust frameworks for identifying gendered experiences, this research addresses a critical methodological gap enabling comparative analysis of women's mobility barriers across diverse urban and cultural contexts.

# iii. Research Methods

This proposed project will combine the various research methods detailed in this section to respond to the articulated research objectives via four corresponding tasks. The research flow is shown in Figure A2 in the Appendix).

We propose a novel approach leveraging social media data to uncover gendered experiences in metro systems globally. Using multilingual keywords, we will analyze user-generated social media content to examine traveling perceptions and service quality dimensions through sentiment analysis and NLP techniques. Case studies in three pre-selecting cities, including Hong Kong, Tehran, and Doha will provide deeper insights into how women's traveling perceptions influence travel behavior and constrain access to high-quality metro services across different demographic and cultural backgrounds.

Our approach integrates digital analytics with transport research to generate valuable knowledge on gendered mobility barriers through three key innovations: leveraging social media analytics to reveal underreported realities of women's transit experiences; developing customized large language models for accurate multilingual sentiment analysis of gendered mobility barriers; and integrating digital analytics with focus group studies through methodological triangulation. The mixed-method framework could directly links women's metro experiences to travel behavior and urban access, generating rich insights.

Task 1.2 Social Media Data Collection and Preprocessing

Social media data will be systematically collected from major platforms Twitter, Facebook, and Instagram. Using keywords related to metro systems in multiple languages, we will retrieve user-generated content spanning a 3-year period with geolocation filtering, temporal parameters, and metadata extraction. The protocol ensures comprehensive coverage of gendered experiences while maintaining ethical data handling practices and compliance with platform terms of service. Collected data will undergo a series of preprocessing including deduplication, language identification, spam/bot removal, and anonymization to protect user privacy. A structured database will be created with standardized fields for text content, metadata, sentiment labels, and gender indicators. The preprocessing pipeline incorporates automated quality checks and manual validation to maintain data integrity throughout the collection and management process.

We will develop a robust infrastructure for processing multilingual social media content, building upon our preliminary work with the TranSenti framework that has demonstrated 78.1% F1 score in identifying transit-related posts and 80.8% F1 score in sentiment analysis accuracy. Our approach will leverage state-of-the-art natural language processing techniques including: (1) hybrid LLM-based filtering using mixture-of-experts architecture with instruction refinement to accurately distinguish genuine passenger experiences from incidental mentions; (2) fine-tuned RoBERTa models with spatio-temporal encoders for context-aware sentiment classification; (3) multilingual processing capabilities with language-specific tokenization (Qwen2.5 for Chinese, multilingual BERT for other languages) and culturally-sensitive text normalization; (4) advanced geoparsing techniques achieving 87.6% accuracy in location extraction with topological constraints; and (5) temporal encoding for peak-hour pattern recognition. The infrastructure will incorporate differential learning rates (2×10⁻⁵ for pre-trained parameters, 1×10⁻³ for new components) and KL-divergence constraints to ensure model consensus, supporting real-time data ingestion and scalable processing across diverse linguistic and cultural contexts.

Task 2: User Profiling and Demographic Inference for Gendered Experience Identification

Task 2.1 Multi-modal Demographic Inference Framework Development

We will develop a comprehensive multi-modal framework for demographic inference and user profiling, specifically designed to identify female metro users and extract authentic gendered travel experiences from social media data. The framework will integrate four complementary approaches: (1) name-based matching using culturally-specific name dictionaries to infer gender and ethnicity across different cultural contexts; (2) linguistic analysis of user posts and self-descriptions to identify gender-associated language patterns and expressions; (3) social network analysis using graph propagation techniques (Label Propagation and Graph Convolutional Networks) to infer demographic attributes based on homophily patterns in followership networks; and (4) profile image analysis using computer vision techniques to extract gender-related visual features.

The name-based matching component will leverage culturally-appropriate name dictionaries for each target region, incorporating local naming conventions and addressing challenges of name ambiguity and cultural specificity (Lasril et al., 2023). For linguistic analysis, we will develop language models that can identify gender-associated linguistic patterns while accounting for cultural variations in expression (Chen et al., 2015). The graph-based propagation methods will enable robust demographic inference even in low-resource scenarios by leveraging network connectivity to propagate information from labeled to unlabeled users (Wang et al., 2019). Profile image analysis will employ state-of-the-art computer vision models trained on diverse demographic datasets to ensure cross-cultural applicability.

Task 2.2 Metro-Specific Content Filtering from raw social media data
Building upon the demographic inference framework, we will develop specialized filtering techniques to distinguish authentic gendered metro experiences from incidental mentions and unrelated content. This will involve creating a hybrid filtering mechanism that combines LLM-driven semantic relevance scoring with domain-specific knowledge about transit contexts (Feng et al., 2024; Ruan et al., 2024). The filtering process will employ instruction-guided optimization to iteratively refine the identification of genuine passenger feedback, ensuring that only posts reflecting actual travel experiences are included in the analysis.

Validation procedures will assess the accuracy of demographic inference across different cultural contexts using metrics for gender classification accuracy, coverage rates, and cross-cultural consistency. We will implement rigorous quality assurance measures including manual validation of inferred demographic attributes, cross-validation with external demographic data sources, and sensitivity analyses to evaluate the robustness of inference methods across different user populations. Ethical guidelines will address potential biases in demographic inference, with special attention to ensuring fair performance across different demographic groups and cultural contexts, while maintaining strict privacy protection protocols for handling sensitive demographic information.

Task 3: Development of Customized Large Language Models for Sentiment Analysis

Task 3.1 Customized LLM Development for Gendered Sentiment Analysis

A customized large language model is essential because generic sentiment analysis tools fail to capture the nuanced expressions of gendered mobility experiences in metro systems. Off-the-shelf models lack domain-specific understanding of transit operations, safety concerns, and culturally specific expressions of harassment and discomfort. They struggle with indirect expressions, multilingual contexts, and spatio-temporal variations that characterize women's metro experiences globally.

The customized LLM will perform specialized functions including: (1) domain-specific sentiment analysis fine-tuned for transit vocabulary and gendered experience indicators; (2) multilingual processing capabilities for diverse cultural contexts; (3) spatio-temporal context integration to associate sentiments with specific operational conditions; (4) recognition of indirect and subtle expressions of gendered experiences; and (5) bias mitigation strategies for equitable performance across demographic groups.

Expected deliverables include a novel Transformer-based architecture optimized for gendered sentiment analysis, a curated multilingual training dataset of 50,000+ annotated social media posts, fine-tuned model weights ready for deployment, comprehensive validation frameworks, and complete technical documentation. This customized approach will overcome limitations of generic tools by providing accurate, context-aware sentiment analysis tailored to gendered mobility barriers.

Task 3.2 Context Integration and Model Validation Framework
The customized LLM will incorporate spatio-temporal encoders to enable context-aware sentiment analysis, learning to associate sentiment expressions with specific operational contexts including time of day, station characteristics, route configurations, and seasonal variations. The model will be trained to recognize how safety perceptions and gendered experiences vary across different global cities, providing nuanced understanding of metro system dynamics and user experiences.

Validation procedures will assess model performance across linguistic, cultural, and operational contexts using metrics for sentiment classification accuracy, cross-cultural consistency, and contextual understanding. Comparative analyses against generic sentiment analysis tools will demonstrate superior performance in capturing gendered experiences, with validation including both quantitative metrics and qualitative assessments by domain experts to ensure practical utility and cultural sensitivity.

Ethical guidelines will be established for LLM development and deployment, addressing potential biases in sentiment analysis across demographic groups and cultural contexts. We will implement bias detection and mitigation strategies to ensure fair and equitable performance, with regular audits for cultural sensitivity, privacy protection, and ethical considerations in handling sensitive content related to harassment and safety concerns, ensuring responsible AI development practices.

Task 4: Integrated Dashboard Development and Focus Group Studies

Task 4.1 Dashboard Development and Data Integration Framework
We will develop an integrated dashboard architecture that combines advanced visualization capabilities with robust data processing to display gendered metro service quality perceptions derived from social media analytics. The dashboard features a modular design with separate panels for spatial analysis, temporal patterns, service quality dimensions, and gender-specific sentiment distributions, employing a responsive web framework for interactive visualizations and spatio-temporal mapping. Advanced visualization techniques include heatmaps for spatial concentrations of gendered traveling concerns, time-series charts for temporal variations, and comparative visualizations highlighting gender disparities, with GIS integration enabling precise mapping of sentiment hotspots to physical metro infrastructure.

A comprehensive data processing pipeline will continuously integrate sentiment analysis outputs from the customized LLM with real-time operational data, handling large-scale social media data streams and performing spatial-temporal aggregation to update visualization components in near real-time. The system incorporates interactive capabilities allowing users to filter data by specific time periods, metro lines, stations, and service dimensions, with dynamic filtering controls enabling real-time exploration of gendered mobility barriers. Advanced analytical features include trend analysis tools, anomaly detection algorithms, and comparative benchmarking across cities, supporting drill-down functionality from aggregate system views to individual station-level analyses.

The deployment strategy includes cloud-based hosting for scalability, secure access controls for different user roles, and comprehensive documentation for sustainable maintenance. The system supports multi-city implementation with configurable parameters, ensuring adaptability to diverse operational environments while maintaining consistent analytical capabilities and visualization standards across all deployment contexts, enabling policymakers to benchmark performance and identify best practices across different urban contexts.

Task 4.2 Focus Group Implementation and Mixed-Methods Integration Framework
We will develop a comprehensive focus group protocol specifically designed to triangulate findings from social media analytics, including semi-structured discussion guides that explore women's lived experiences, safety perceptions, and mobility constraints in metro systems. Cultural adaptation is a critical component, with protocols tailored to local contexts in each selected city to ensure cultural sensitivity and relevance. A stratified sampling approach will recruit diverse participants representing different demographic profiles, travel patterns, and metro usage frequencies, targeting regular female metro users across various age groups, socioeconomic backgrounds, and occupational categories.

Structured focus group discussions will be conducted in each of the three selected cities with 50-100 participants per group and multiple sessions to ensure data saturation, facilitated by trained moderators with expertise in gender and transportation research and assisted by local collaborators for cultural and linguistic mediation. Data collection includes audio recording, verbatim transcription, simultaneous translation, and documentation of observational notes to capture nuanced expressions of gendered mobility experiences.

A systematic triangulation framework will integrate quantitative findings from social media analytics with qualitative insights from focus groups, employing both deductive and inductive coding approaches for thematic analysis and pattern identification. This mixed-methods approach validates the customized LLM's performance in capturing authentic gendered experiences while critically examining discrepancies to identify methodological limitations. The integrated findings will inform development of contextually appropriate policy recommendations and intervention strategies, with knowledge translation activities including co-design workshops with policymakers and transit operators to ensure research findings are translated into actionable interventions that address specific needs identified through both digital and qualitative methodologies, creating sustainable impact on metro system improvements and gendered mobility equity.

## Conclusion

This research addresses a critical gap in transport equity by investigating gendered mobility barriers for women in metro systems through an innovative social media analytics approach. By leveraging large-scale digital data across diverse cultural contexts, the study will generate insights into the realities of women's transit experiences. The proposed methods overcomes traditional limitations of underreporting and provides a scalable framework for capturing authentic, real-time perceptions of safety and service quality across different socioeconomic and cultural environments.

The integration of customized large language models for multilingual sentiment analysis, interactive dashboard visualization, and mixed-methods case studies will represent an advancement in transport research. This comprehensive approach enables both broad comparative analysis and deep contextual understanding, bridging quantitative patterns with qualitative narratives, to produce knowledge for policymakers and transit operators, facilitating evidence-based interventions to enhance safety, inclusivity, and gender equity in metro systems.

Ultimately, this study contributes to broader societal goals of gender equality and social inclusion by empowering women with more comfortable and accessible urban mobility. The findings will inform strategies to address spatial and temporal hotspots of gendered traveling concerns, while the developed tools and frameworks will establish new standards for ongoing monitoring and evaluation. By translating digital insights into concrete policy recommendations, this research promises to create more equitable urban environments that enable women's full participation in economic, educational, and social opportunities.
