## Abstract (max. 400 words)

Urbanization has significantly improved transport infrastructure worldwide, yet metro systems do not serve all population groups equally. Women face distinct mobility challenges including voyeurism, sexual harassment, and disrespect for breastfeeding-issues that remain largely invisible due to social norms, cultural pressures, and fear of stigma. This problem is particularly acute in developing countries where limited surveillance exacerbates women's vulnerability.

Traditional research methods such as questionnaire surveys, interviews, and focus groups have provided valuable qualitative insights into women's mobility experiences, but they face significant limitations in scale, real-time data capture, and the ability to uncover unspoken realities across diverse cultural contexts. These methods often suffer from sampling biases, social desirability bias, and limited geographical coverage, particularly in developing countries where official reporting mechanisms are inadequate. Moreover, traditional approaches struggle to capture the spontaneous, real-time nature of gendered experiences that often go unreported due to cultural pressures.

This research employs an innovative social media analytics approach to overcome these limitations and capture and analyze unspoken, real-world experiences of women in metro systems globally. We propose a mixed-method framework that: (1) leverages multilingual social media data mining to uncover gendered experiences across diverse contexts; (2) develops customized large language models for accurate sentiment analysis and nuanced interpretation of gendered mobility barriers; and (3) integrates digital analytics with focus group study in three selected Asian cities - Hong Kong, Tehran and Doha.

Our approach advances transport equity research through three key innovations: harnessing social media analytics to reveal underreported realities, building culturally sensitive language models for multilingual analysis, and establishing methodological triangulation through mixed-methods design. The study will generate a unique global dataset of gendered metro experiences, provide actionable insights for policymakers and transit operators, and contribute to creating safer, more equitable urban environments that empower women's mobility and access to opportunities.

## (a) Project Objectives (max. 800 words)

Urbanization has significantly improved transport infrastructure and mobility in cities worldwide, with an increasing number of cities operating or planning metro systems. However, advanced public transport systems like metros do not serve all population groups equally. The transport equity literature has shown that different social strata benefit unevenly from transit services. Women, in particular, often face distinct mobility challenges compared with men. Many of these challenges-such as voyeurism, sexual harassment, and the disrespect of breastfeeding-are underreported due to social norms, cultural pressures, and fear of stigma. As a result, they remain largely invisible to passengers, operators, and policymakers, and receive limited coverage in mainstream media. This issue is particularly acute in developing countries, where limited surveillance and policing exacerbate the problem. To address this gap, the proposed research will use a social media analytics approach to capture and analyse unspoken, real-world experiences of women in metro systems globally. Theoretically, it contributes to the transport equity discourse by foregrounding gendered mobility barriers. Methodologically, it introduces a novel application of social media analytics in transport research. Empirically, it will build a unique dataset of gendered metro experiences across diverse contexts. Practically, the study will provide actionable insights for policymakers and transit operators to improve safety, inclusivity, and equity in metro systems.

Achieving transport equity requires addressing the needs of socially disadvantaged groups, including women. A safer and more inclusive travel experience can increase women's mobility, empowering them to access employment, education, and social opportunities across the city and beyond. From a societal perspective, improving women's experiences in public transit not only enhances their well-being but also supports broader goals of social inclusion and gender equality. In advanced economies such as Japan and Hong Kong, urban metros are the backbone of the transport network, carrying the majority of daily trips. Yet even in these systems, women often feel unsafe, particularly at night or in sparsely populated areas and lines. In contrast, in cities where metros are less dominant and public transport use is lower, reduced crowds do not necessarily translate into safety. Limited public surveillance can increase women's vulnerability to harassment and other harmful acts. By systematically addressing these challenges, this research will advance knowledge on gender and mobility, inform evidence-based interventions, and ultimately contribute to creating safer, more equitable urban environments that benefit society as a whole.

## Objective 2: To develop user profiling techniques for identifying gendered experiences and extracting authentic metro-related social media data

The second objective focuses on developing advanced user profiling and demographic inference techniques to identify female metro users and extract authentic gendered travel experiences from social media data. Building on demographic inference methods, we will create a multi-modal framework that integrates name-based matching, linguistic analysis, social network propagation, and profile image analysis to infer user gender and demographic attributes across diverse cultural contexts.

## Objective 3: To develop a large language model for sentiment analysis of gendered experiences on the metro systems

The third objective focuses on developing a customized large language model (LLM) specifically designed for sentiment analysis of gendered experiences in metro systems. We will create a specialized LLM architecture that integrates multilingual processing capabilities with domain-specific knowledge about transit operations and gendered mobility patterns. This model will be trained on carefully curated social media data from the selected cities, incorporating both textual content and associated spatio-temporal metadata to enable context-aware sentiment classification.

Our approach will leverage sentiment models fine-tuned with transit-specific vocabulary and gendered experience indicators. The model will be designed to recognize expressions of safety concerns, sexual harassment experiences, comfort levels, and service quality perceptions that are often expressed indirectly or through culturally specific linguistic patterns. By incorporating spatio-temporal encoders, the model will learn to associate sentiments with specific operational contexts (e.g., peak hours, particular stations or lines), enabling accurate interpretation of gendered experiences across diverse cultural contexts.

## Objective 4: To develop an integrated dashboard for visualizing gendered metro experiences

An integrated platform will be developed to visualize spatio-temporal patterns of gendered service quality perceptions while incorporating qualitative insights from direct engagement with women metro users: the dashboard will feature interactive functions for displaying sentiment scores, temporal variations (hourly, daily, seasonal), and gender-specific sentiment distributions, using heatmaps for spatial traveling concern concentrations and comparative visualizations to highlight gender disparities.

The focus group component will adopt a mixed-methods approach to validate and contextualize social media analytics insights. Through structured discussions in three selected cities, quantitative findings will be triangulated with rich qualitative data, exploring how women articulate metro experience perceptions, how these perceptions are interpreted and their links to actual travel behaviors, and how perceived barriers impact women's access to urban opportunities.

## Research Project Statement (max. 7 pages)

## i. Research Context

## (a) Gendered Mobility Barriers in Metro Systems

Women face distinct mobility challenges in public transit systems worldwide, with research consistently demonstrating pervasive yet underreported gendered barriers due to social norms and fear of stigma (Mazumder & Pokharel, 2018; King et al., 2021). These challenges encompass profound safety concerns, with women reporting significantly higher levels of fear and insecurity compared to men in metro environments (Ouali et al., 2020; Lindkvist, 2024). Historical and socio-cultural factors deeply influence women's mobility patterns. Becker's (2019) work reveals how historical norms continue to shape contemporary mobility restrictions, while Field and Vyborny (2022) demonstrate that safety and social acceptability constitute primary barriers to women's mobility, highlighting the importance of gender-sensitive transport services.

The manifestations of gendered barriers vary across contexts but share common themes of harassment and restricted access. From sexual violence in Bangladesh (Mazumder & Pokharel, 2018) to heightened insecurity in Latin America (Infante-Vargas & Boyer, 2021; Busco et al., 2022), women become "captive travelers" forced to prioritize safety over affordability through extensive behavioral adaptations. Beyond safety concerns, women face significant time poverty exacerbated by entrenched gender roles and caregiving responsibilities (Zehba M. P. et al., 2025). This time poverty, combined with the emotional "affective ordeals" of unsafe conditions (Roy et al., 2024), creates complex mobility constraints of women that traditional research methods often fail to capture comprehensively.

We conduct a systematic literature review and reveal a critical scarcity of questionnaire surveys and focus group studies specifically addressing women's mobility barriers in metro systems. Globally, only a handful of surveys have been conducted, with most studies employing small sample sizes (typically 8-38 participants for qualitative interviews and focus groups) and limited geographical coverage. We identified only 3 major surveys with sample sizes exceeding 500 respondents across the entire Asian continent: a study in Hyderabad, India (583 women commuters), research across eight European countries (1406 female travelers), and limited surveys in cities in China, which represents a severe underrepresentation regarding the research topic. Specifically, Kerzhner et al. (2018) used only 8 interviews and 38 focus group participants in Jerusalem, Kacharo et al. (2022) employed 24 focus group members in Hawassa, Ethiopia, Infante-Vargas & Boyer (2021) conducted only 11 interviews in Saltillo, Mexico, and Sil et al. (2023) highlighted the prevalence of small-sample studies across South and Southeast Asia. Roy et al. (2024) employed visual surveys in Kolkata, and most studies rely on samples of 24-38 focus group participants. Quantitative surveys with robust statistical power are exceptionally rare, particularly those employing mixed-methods designs that could capture both the scale and nuance of gendered mobility barriers. This research vacuum is particularly acute in developing Asian countries where cultural norms and fear of stigma further suppress reporting of sensitive issues like sexual harassment and safety concerns.

## (b) Methodological Innovations in Social Media Analytics

The limitations of traditional methods in capturing underreported gendered experiences have prompted innovative approaches using social media analytics (SMA). SMA offers real-time insights into public perceptions, complementing traditional survey and qualitative methods (Thelwall, 2017; Sufi, 2023). In transport research, SMA has demonstrated significant potential for uncovering service quality issues. Luo and He(2020,2021)pioneered social media mining for transit services, revealing temporal and spatial variations in perceptions while identifying distinct gender gaps in concerns-with women focusing more on comfort and safety. The methodological landscape is rapidly evolving with advanced computational techniques. Deep learning approaches (Hayat et al., 2019) and transformer models like DistilBERT (Alharbi & Haq, 2024) have enhanced processing power and predictive accuracy for complex text analysis tasks in transportation contexts. The emergence of Large Language Models represents a transformative advancement. Specialized LLMs fine-tuned on domain-specific data demonstrate potential for understanding nuanced expressions in specific contexts, with multimodal capabilities enhancing analysis of visual and textual information interplay (Radwan et al., 2024; Y. Li et al., 2023).

## (c) User Profiling Challenge

A critical methodological challenge in gendered mobility research is accurate demographic identification, particularly gender attribution from social media data. Traditional approaches relying on self-reported gender or platform data face limitations from privacy concerns, incomplete profiles, and cultural variations in gender expression, leading to a fundamental barrier to distinguishing authentic gendered experiences from general transit feedback (Liu et al., 2023). Recent advances in multi-modal demographic inference offer promising solutions. Techniques combining name-based prediction, linguistic style analysis, social network propagation, and profile image analysis demonstrate improved accuracy in cross-cultural gender attribution (Wang & Zhang, 2022). The integration of advanced user profiling with sentiment analysis represents a novel contribution to transport equity research. By developing robust frameworks for identifying gendered experiences, this research addresses a critical methodological gap enabling comparative analysis of women's mobility barriers across diverse urban and cultural contexts.

## ii. Research Questions

This study addresses the following research questions to systematically investigate gendered mobility barriers in metro systems through social media analytics:

Q1: How do gendered perceptions of service quality in metro systems vary across different economic, cultural, and urban contexts globally?

Q2: To what extent can customized large language models accurately identify and classify nuanced expressions of gendered mobility barriers in multilingual social media data?

Q3: What spatio-temporal patterns of gendered traveling concerns emerge from social media data, and how do these patterns correlate with specific metro service, infrastructure, and operational characteristics?

Q4: How do women's self-reported experiences in focus groups validate and contextualize the gendered mobility barriers identified through large-scale social media analytics?

Our city selection methodology employs a systematic filtering process from a comprehensive database of 186 global metro cities. We first collect data across five dimensions: GDP per capita (World Bank 2023), Gender Inequality Index (UNDP 2023), internet penetration rates (World Bank/ITU 2023), urban characteristics (population density and service sector employment from Our World in Data and ILO 2022-2023), and metro-specific metrics (ridership patterns and operational characteristics from metro operators). Final selection balances analytical requirements with practical considerations including social media content volume (minimum 10,000 annual metro-related posts), linguistic diversity, geographic distribution, and operational accessibility for research partnerships.

This multidimensional approach ensures comprehensive representation across different development levels and gender equality contexts, enabling robust comparative analysis of gendered mobility barriers across varied socioeconomic environments. ## Task 1.2 Social Media Data Collection and Preprocessing

Social media data will be systematically collected from major platforms Twitter, Facebook, and Instagram. Using keywords related to metro systems in multiple languages, we will retrieve user-generated content spanning a 3-year period with geolocation filtering, temporal parameters, and metadata extraction. The protocol ensures comprehensive coverage of gendered experiences while maintaining ethical data handling practices and compliance with platform terms of service. Collected data will undergo a series of preprocessing including deduplication, language identification, spam/bot removal, and anonymization to protect user privacy. A structured database will be created with standardized fields for text content, metadata, sentiment labels, and gender indicators. The preprocessing pipeline incorporates automated quality checks and manual validation to maintain data integrity throughout the collection and management process.

We will develop a robust infrastructure for processing multilingual social media content, building upon our preliminary work that has demonstrated ${78.1}\% \mathrm{F}1$ score in identifying transit-related posts from raw social media data and ${80.8}\% \mathrm{\;F}1$ score in sentiment analysis accuracy. Our approach will leverage state-of-the-art natural language processing techniques including: (1) hybrid LLM-based filtering using mixture-of-experts architecture with instruction refinement to accurately distinguish genuine passenger experiences from incidental mentions; (2) fine-tuned RoBERTa models with spatio-temporal encoders for context-aware sentiment classification; (3) multilingual processing capabilities with language-specific tokenization (multilingual Qwen embedding model) and culturally-sensitive text normalization; (4) advanced geoparsing techniques achieving 87.6% accuracy in location extraction with topological constraints; and (5) temporal encoding for peak-hour pattern recognition. The infrastructure will support real-time data ingestion, batch processing capabilities, and scalable storage solutions to handle large volumes of social media data from diverse global contexts, ensuring efficient processing across different operational requirements.

## Task 2: User Profiling and Demographic Inference for Gendered Experience Identification

## Task 2.1 Multi-modal Demographic Inference Framework Development

We will develop a comprehensive multi-modal framework for demographic inference and user profiling, specifically designed to identify female metro users and extract authentic gendered travel experiences from social media data. The framework will integrate four complementary approaches: (1) name-based matching using culturally-specific name dictionaries to infer gender and ethnicity across different cultural contexts; (2) linguistic analysis of user posts and self-descriptions to identify gender-associated language patterns and expressions; (3) social network analysis using graph propagation techniques (Label Propagation and Graph Convolutional Networks) to infer demographic attributes based on homophily patterns in followership networks; and (4) profile image analysis using computer vision techniques to extract gender-related visual features.

The name-based matching component will leverage culturally-appropriate name dictionaries for each target region, incorporating local naming conventions and addressing challenges of name ambiguity and cultural specificity (Lasril et al., 2023). For linguistic analysis, we will develop language models that can identify gender-associated linguistic patterns while accounting for cultural variations in expression (Chen et al., 2015). The graph-based propagation methods will enable robust demographic inference by leveraging network connectivity to propagate information from labeled to unlabeled users (Wang et al., 2019). Profile image analysis will employ state-of-the-art computer vision models trained on diverse demographic datasets to ensure cross-cultural applicability.

## Task 2.2 Metro-Specific Content Filtering from raw social media data

Building upon the demographic inference framework, we will develop specialized filtering techniques to distinguish authentic gendered metro experiences from incidental mentions and unrelated content. This will involve creating a hybrid filtering mechanism that combines LLM-driven semantic relevance scoring with domain-specific knowledge about transit contexts (Feng et al., 2024; Ruan et al., 2024). The filtering process will employ instruction-guided optimization to iteratively refine the identification of genuine passenger feedback, ensuring that only posts reflecting actual travel experiences are included in the analysis.

Validation procedures will assess the accuracy of demographic inference across different cultural contexts using metrics for gender classification accuracy, coverage rates, and cross-cultural consistency. We will implement rigorous quality assurance measures including manual validation of inferred demographic attributes, cross-validation with external demographic data sources, and sensitivity analyses to evaluate the robustness of inference methods across different user populations. Ethical guidelines will address potential biases in demographic inference, with special attention to ensuring fair performance across different demographic groups and cultural contexts, while maintaining strict privacy protection protocols for handling sensitive demographic information.

## Task 3: Development of Customized Large Language Models for Sentiment Analysis Task 3.1 LLM Architecture Development and Training Data Curation

A customized large language model is essential because generic sentiment analysis tools fail to capture the nuanced expressions of gendered mobility experiences in metro systems. Off-the-shelf models lack domain-specific understanding of transit operations, safety concerns, and culturally specific expressions of harassment and discomfort (Luo & He, 2021; Radwan et al., 2024). They struggle with indirect expressions, multilingual contexts, and spatio-temporal variations that characterize women's metro experiences globally.

The customized LLM will perform specialized functions including: (1) domain-specific sentiment analysis fine-tuned for transit vocabulary and gendered experience indicators, building on state-of-the-art approaches like RoBERTa (Liu et al., 2019); (2) multilingual processing capabilities for diverse cultural contexts, leveraging techniques demonstrated in multilingual sentiment analysis (Sufi, 2023); (3) spatio-temporal context integration to associate sentiments with specific operational conditions; (4) recognition of indirect and subtle expressions of gendered experiences; and (5) bias mitigation strategies for equitable performance across demographic groups.

Expected deliverables include a novel Transformer-based architecture optimized for gendered sentiment analysis, a curated multilingual training dataset of annotated social media posts, fine-tuned model weights ready for deployment, comprehensive validation frameworks, and complete technical documentation. This customized approach will overcome limitations of generic tools by providing accurate, context-aware sentiment analysis tailored to gendered mobility barriers , following the methodological advancements in domain-specific LLM fine-tuning demonstrated by Li et al. (2023) and S et al. (2025).

The model will be trained to recognize how safety perceptions and gendered experiences vary across different global cities, providing nuanced understanding of metro system dynamics and user experiences.

Validation procedures will assess model performance across linguistic, cultural, and operational contexts using metrics for sentiment classification accuracy, cross-cultural consistency, and contextual understanding. ## Task 4: Focus Group Studies for Gendered Mobility Experience Task 4.1 Focus Group Implementation in Three Selected Cities

We will develop a comprehensive focus group protocol specifically designed to triangulate findings from social media analytics, including semi-structured discussion guides that explore women's lived experiences, safety perceptions, and mobility constraints in metro systems.
