Title: 
Gender and Transport Equity in Asia: A Comparative Study of Women’s Mobility in Metro Transit through Social Media Analytics



Principal Investigator: Sylvia He
The Chinese University of Hong Kong



Keywords: Transport Equity, Metro Transit, Gendered Mobility, Social Media Analytics, Large Language Models 
 
Research Project Statement (max. 7 pages)

i.	Research Context 

 (a) Gendered Mobility Barriers in Metro Systems
Women face distinct mobility challenges in public transit systems worldwide, with research consistently demonstrating pervasive yet underreported gendered barriers due to social norms and fear of stigma (Mazumder & Pokharel, 2018; King et al., 2021). These challenges encompass profound safety concerns, with women reporting significantly higher levels of fear and insecurity compared to men in metro environments (Ouali et al., 2020; Ceccato et al., 2024). Historical and socio-cultural factors deeply influence women's mobility patterns. Panjwani (2018) reveals how socio-cultural norms continue to shape contemporary mobility restrictions in developing contexts, while Field and Vyborny (2022) demonstrate that safety and social acceptability constitute primary barriers to women's mobility, highlighting the importance of gender-sensitive transport services.

The manifestations of gendered barriers vary across contexts but share common themes of harassment and restricted access. From sexual violence in Bangladesh (Mazumder & Pokharel, 2018) to heightened insecurity in Latin America (Infante-Vargas & Boyer, 2021; Joshi et al., 2022), women are compelled to restrict their mobility and prioritize safety through extensive behavioral adaptations. Beyond safety concerns, women face significant time poverty exacerbated by entrenched gender roles and caregiving responsibilities (Hail & McQuaid, 2021). This time poverty, combined with the challenges of accessing equitable transport services (Pourhashem et al., 2022), creates complex mobility constraints of women that traditional research methods often fail to capture comprehensively.

Our systematic review indicates a predominant reliance on single-method designs that constrain validity, coverage, and transferability of findings. Many studies rely either on cross-sectional questionnaires that are vulnerable to recall and social desirability biases (Kerzhner et al., 2018) and offer limited spatio-temporal coverage (Ceccato et al., 2024), or on stand-alone qualitative interviews/focus groups that provide rich narratives yet make it difficult to assess prevalence, support cross-city comparison, and link mechanisms to patterns at scale (Sil et al., 2023). Single-method approaches also struggle to surface underreported and culturally sensitive experiences—particularly salient in Asian contexts where stigma suppresses disclosure and they lack triangulation across independent data sources. Addressing this limitation calls for a mixed-methods strategy that integrates large-scale quantitative evidence (e.g., digital trace and survey data) with qualitative insights (e.g., culturally sensitive focus groups) to achieve both breadth and depth, enable cross-context comparisons, and enhance the credibility and actionability of evidence on gendered mobility barriers in metro systems (Ceccato et al. 2024).

(b) Methodological Innovations in Social Media Analytics
The limitations of traditional methods in capturing underreported gendered experiences have prompted innovative approaches using social media analytics (SMA). SMA offers near real-time insights into public perceptions, complementing traditional survey and qualitative methods (Thelwall, 2017; Yigitcanlar et al., 2020). Importantly, social media platforms serve as vital sites for the circulation of public discourse about sexual harassment and gender-based violence in public transport, providing spaces where women can share experiences that might otherwise remain silenced. As one of our Co-Is, Tan (2023) demonstrates in her analysis of feminist digital activism in China, social media platforms enable creative feminist strategies to emerge for addressing sexual assault and harassment in public transit, raising awareness, and building solidarity among women facing similar challenges. In transport research, SMA has demonstrated significant potential for uncovering service quality issues. Luo and He (2020, 2021) pioneered social media mining for transit services, revealing temporal and spatial variations in perceptions while identifying distinct gender gaps in concerns—with women focusing more on comfort and safety. The methodological landscape is rapidly evolving with advanced computational techniques. Deep learning approaches and sentiment analysis frameworks (Collins et al., 2013; El-Diraby et al., 2019) have enhanced processing power and predictive accuracy for complex text analysis tasks in transportation contexts. The emergence of Large Language Models represents a transformative advancement. Specialized LLMs fine-tuned on domain-specific data demonstrate potential for understanding nuanced expressions in specific contexts, with multimodal capabilities enhancing analysis of visual and textual information interplay (Li et al., 2023; Tian et al., 2020).
 
(c) User Profiling Challenge
A critical methodological challenge in gendered mobility research is accurate demographic identification, particularly gender attributes from social media data. Traditional approaches relying on self-reported gender or platform data face limitations from privacy concerns, incomplete profiles, and cultural variations in gender expression, leading to a fundamental barrier to distinguishing authentic gendered experiences from general transit feedback (Liu et al., 2023). Recent advances in multi-modal demographic inference offer promising solutions. Techniques combining name-based prediction, linguistic style analysis, social network propagation, and profile image analysis demonstrate improved accuracy in cross-cultural gender attributes (Wang & Zhang., 2022). The integration of advanced user profiling with sentiment analysis represents a novel contribution to transport equity research. By developing robust frameworks for identifying gendered experiences, this research addresses a critical methodological gap enabling comparative analysis of women's mobility barriers across diverse urban and cultural contexts.

ii.	Research Questions 
This study addresses the following five research questions:

Q1. How can we systematically select global metro cities that represent diverse economic, social, and digital contexts, and what insights can a global comparative analysis reveal about the visibility of women’s experiences in digital data?
Addressed through Task 1, this question develops a multidimensional framework (GDP per capita, Gender Inequality Index, digital infrastructure, urban density, and metro usage) to identify representative cities worldwide. The global scope enables systematic cross-city comparison, while also clarifying how socio-economic and institutional conditions influence the presence—and absence—of women’s voices in digital data.

Q2. How can female metro users be reliably identified in multilingual social media environments, and what do their narratives reveal about commonalities and differences in gendered mobility experiences across contexts?
Task 2 builds demographic inference and content filtering frameworks to extract authentic experiences from multilingual sources. Beyond improving technical accuracy, this question enables comparative analysis of women’s narratives across cities and cultures, identifying both shared challenges and context-specific barriers in metro travel.

Q3. To what extent can large language models (LLMs) classify and interpret gendered mobility barriers across different linguistic and cultural settings, and what new analytical perspectives can they contribute compared with conventional approaches?
Task 3 designs and validates domain-specific LLMs for sentiment and barrier detection in transit contexts. The contribution lies in testing whether AI can uncover cross-linguistic and cross-cultural patterns that remain hidden in traditional methods, thus advancing methodological innovation in gender-sensitive urban research.

Q4. How can focus group studies in Hong Kong, Delhi, and Istanbul validate, challenge, or enrich insights derived from social media analytics, and what does this triangulation reveal about the strengths and blind spots of digital data?
Task 4 conducts focus groups to address representativeness and contextual gaps. The comparative design across three cities allows systematic evaluation of how digital traces align with lived experiences, clarifying both the promise and the limitations of social media analytics.

Q5. How can an integrated dashboard system best visualize and compare gendered mobility patterns across cities, and what analytical value does it offer for policymakers in monitoring women’s experiences, identifying hotspots, and evaluating interventions?
Task 5 develops a dashboard that integrates social media and focus group findings into accessible visualizations. Beyond technical outputs, the dashboard provides comparative analytics to reveal hotspots, trends, and outcomes of interventions, offering policymakers and operators a practical tool for advancing gender equity in metro systems..

iii.	Research Methods
This proposed project will combine the various research methods detailed in this section to respond to the articulated research objectives via four corresponding tasks. The research flow
is shown in Figure A1 in the Appendix). We propose a novel approach leveraging social media data to uncover gendered experiences in metro systems across Asia. Using multilingual keywords, we will analyze user-generated social media content to examine traveling perceptions and service quality dimensions through sentiment analysis and NLP techniques. Case studies in three pre-selected Asian cities will provide deeper insights into how women’s traveling perceptions influence travel behavior and constrain access to high-quality metro services across different demographic and cultural backgrounds within the Asian context.

Task 1: Global City Selection and Social Media Data Collection
Task 1.1  Global Selection of Metro Cities
We will employ a comprehensive multidimensional framework for selecting cities with metro systems that represent diverse economic, social, and digital contexts. The selection criteria encompass five key dimensions: economic development (GDP per capita), gender equality (Gender Inequality Index), digital infrastructure (internet penetration rates), urban characteristics (population density, service sector employment), and transport-specific metrics (metro usage patterns). This approach ensures representation across different development levels and gender equality contexts, enabling comparative analysis of gendered mobility barriers across varied socioeconomic environments. We will employ a multidimensional framework for selecting cities with metro systems that represent diverse economic, social, and digital contexts.

Our city selection methodology employs a systematic filtering process from a comprehensive database of 186 metro cities. We will first collect data across five dimensions: GDP per capita (World Bank 2023), Gender Inequality Index (UNDP 2023), internet penetration rates (World Bank/ITU 2023), urban characteristics (population density and service sector employment from Our World in Data and ILO 2022-2023), and metro-specific metrics (ridership patterns and operational characteristics from metro operators). Primary filtering criteria include internet penetration ≥60%, metro operation ≥5 years, daily ridership ≥100,000 passengers, and availability of gender-disaggregated transport data to ensure research feasibility and adequate social media content generation. From the filtered pool, cities are categorized into six analytical quadrants based on indicator combinations to ensure representative coverage across different development and gender equality contexts. Final selection balances analytical requirements with practical considerations including social media content volume (minimum 10,000 annual metro-related posts), linguistic diversity, geographic distribution, and operational accessibility for research partnerships.

This multidimensional approach ensures comprehensive representation across different development levels and gender equality contexts, enabling robust comparative analysis of gendered mobility barriers across varied socioeconomic environments. The framework is designed to capture the complex interplay between economic conditions, social structures, digital accessibility, urban form, and transit system attributes that collectively shape women's metro experiences. Preliminary analysis (see Figure A2) demonstrates the distribution of cities with metro systems across GDP and GII quadrants, revealing opportunities for comparative analysis across different development levels and gender equality contexts (three cities circled are related to Task 4 in this project).

Task 1.2 Social Media Data Collection and Preprocessing
Social media data will be systematically collected from major platforms, such as Twitter, Facebook, and Instagram. Using keywords related to metro systems in multiple languages, we will retrieve user-generated content spanning a 3-year period (from 2022 to 2024) with geolocation filtering, temporal parameters, and metadata extraction. The protocol ensures comprehensive coverage of gendered experiences while maintaining ethical data handling practices and compliance with platform terms of service. Collected data will undergo a series of preprocessing including deduplication, language identification, spam/bot removal, and anonymization to protect user privacy. A structured database will be created with standardized fields for text content, metadata, sentiment labels, and gender indicators. The preprocessing pipeline incorporates automated quality checks and manual validation to maintain data integrity throughout the collection and management process.

We will develop a robust infrastructure for processing multilingual social media content, building upon our preliminary work that has demonstrated 78.1% F1 score in identifying transit-related posts from raw social media data and 80.8% F1 score in sentiment analysis accuracy. Our approach will leverage state-of-the-art natural language processing techniques including: (1) hybrid LLM-based filtering using mixture-of-experts architecture with instruction refinement to accurately distinguish genuine passenger experiences from incidental mentions; (2) fine-tuned RoBERTa models with spatio-temporal encoders for context-aware sentiment classification; (3) multilingual processing capabilities with language-specific tokenization (multilingual Qwen embedding model) and culturally-sensitive text normalization; (4) advanced geoparsing techniques achieving 87.6% accuracy in location extraction with topological constraints; and (5) temporal encoding for peak-hour pattern recognition. The infrastructure will support real-time data ingestion, batch processing capabilities, and scalable storage solutions to handle large volumes of social media data from diverse contexts, ensuring efficient processing across different operational requirements.

Task 2: User Profiling and Demographic Inference 
Task 2.1 Multi-modal Demographic Inference Framework 
We will develop a comprehensive multi-modal framework for demographic inference and user profiling, specifically designed to identify female metro users and extract authentic gendered travel experiences from social media data. The framework will integrate four complementary approaches: (1) name-based matching using culturally-specific name dictionaries to infer gender and ethnicity across different cultural contexts (Wang & Zhang, 2022); (2) linguistic analysis of user posts and self-descriptions to identify gender-associated language patterns and expressions; (3) social network analysis using graph propagation techniques (Label Propagation and Graph Convolutional Networks) to infer demographic attributes based on homophily patterns in followership networks; and (4) profile image analysis using computer vision techniques to extract gender-related visual features (Liang et al., 2018).

The name-based matching component will leverage name dictionaries for each target region, incorporating local naming conventions and addressing challenges of name ambiguity and cultural specificity (Lasri et al., 2023). For linguistic analysis, we will develop language models that can identify gender-associated linguistic patterns while accounting for cultural variations in expression (Chen et al., 2015). The graph-based propagation methods will enable robust demographic inference by leveraging network connectivity to propagate information from labeled to unlabeled users (Wang et al., 2019). Profile analysis will employ state-of-the-art computer vision models trained on diverse demographic datasets.

Task 2.2 Metro-Specific Content Filtering 
Building upon the demographic inference framework, we will develop specialized filtering techniques to distinguish authentic gendered metro experiences from incidental mentions and unrelated content. This will involve creating a hybrid filtering mechanism that combines LLM-driven semantic relevance scoring with domain-specific knowledge about transit contexts (Goodchild, 2007; Schweitzer, 2014). The filtering process will employ instruction-guided optimization to iteratively refine the identification of genuine passenger feedback, ensuring that only posts reflecting actual travel experiences are included in the analysis.

Validation procedures will assess the accuracy of demographic inference across different cultural contexts using metrics for gender classification accuracy, coverage rates, and cross-cultural consistency. We will implement rigorous quality assurance measures including manual validation of inferred demographic attributes, cross-validation with external demographic data sources, and sensitivity study to evaluate the robustness of inference methods across different user populations. Ethical guidelines will address potential biases in demographic inference, with special attention to ensuring fair performance across different demographic groups and cultural contexts, while maintaining strict privacy protection protocols for handling sensitive demographic information.

Task 3: Customized LLMs for Gendered Mobility Analysis
Task 3.1 LLM Architecture Development and Training Data Curation
A customized large language model is essential because generic sentiment analysis tools fail to capture the nuanced expressions of gendered mobility experiences in metro systems. Off-the-shelf models lack domain-specific understanding of transit operations, safety concerns, and culturally specific expressions of harassment and discomfort (Luo & He, 2021; Rashidi et al., 2017). They struggle with indirect expressions, multilingual contexts, and spatio-temporal variations that characterize women’s metro experiences globally.

The customized LLM will perform specialized functions including: (1) domain-specific sentiment analysis fine-tuned for transit vocabulary and gendered experience indicators, building on state-of-the-art approaches like RoBERTa (Liu et al., 2019) and SKEP (Tian et al., 2020); (2) multilingual processing capabilities for diverse cultural contexts, leveraging techniques demonstrated in multilingual sentiment analysis (Grant-Muller et al., 2014); (3) spatio-temporal context integration to associate sentiments with specific operational conditions; (4) recognition of indirect and subtle expressions of gendered experiences; and (5) bias mitigation strategies for equitable performance across demographic groups.

Expected deliverables include a novel Transformer-based architecture optimized for gendered sentiment analysis, a curated multilingual training dataset of annotated social media posts, fine-tuned model weights ready for deployment, comprehensive validation frameworks, and complete technical documentation. This customized approach will overcome limitations of generic tools by providing accurate, context-aware sentiment analysis tailored to gendered mobility barriers, following the methodological advancements in domain-specific LLM fine-tuning demonstrated by Li et al. (2023) and Tian et al. (2020).

Task 3.2 Context Integration and Validation 
The customized LLM will incorporate spatio-temporal encoders to enable context-aware sentiment analysis, learning to associate sentiment expressions with specific operational contexts including time of day, station characteristics, route configurations, and seasonal variations. The model will be trained to recognize how safety perceptions and gendered experiences vary across different cities, providing nuanced understanding of metro system dynamics and user experiences.

Validation procedures will assess model performance across linguistic, cultural, and operational contexts using metrics for sentiment classification accuracy, cross-cultural consistency, and contextual understanding. Comparative analysis against generic sentiment analysis tools will demonstrate superior performance in capturing gendered experiences, with validation including both quantitative metrics and qualitative assessments by domain experts to ensure practical utility and cultural sensitivity.

Ethical guidelines will be established for LLM development and deployment, addressing potential biases in sentiment analysis across demographic groups and cultural contexts. We will implement bias detection and mitigation strategies to ensure fair and equitable performance, with regular audits for cultural sensitivity, privacy protection, and ethical considerations in handling sensitive content related to harassment and safety concerns, ensuring responsible AI development practices.

Task 4: Focus Group Studies with LLM Support
Task 4.1 Validation and Enrichment of Social Media Insights
While social media analytics offers unprecedented scale and real-time signals, qualitative focus groups provide complementary strengths: they surface tacit norms and sensitive experiences, generate new insights through participant-to-participant dialogue, and allow observation of nonverbal cues and consensus dynamics (Casey, 2015; Nyumba et al., 2018). We will conduct focus groups in three cities—Hong Kong, Delhi, and Istanbul (Figure A2)—to complement social media analytics through two mechanisms: (1) gap identification, where focus groups explore experiences absent from digital data due to barriers such as limited online access or cultural stigma; and (2) contextual interpretation, where participants provide cultural frameworks for interpreting women’s metro experiences.

Recruitment, Sampling, and Ethics
Each focus group will include up to eight participants recruited to reflect diversity in age, occupation, socioeconomic background, and frequency of metro use. Particular attention will be given to engaging participants who may be more vulnerable to harassment or exclusion, while ensuring a safe and supportive environment. Participation will be voluntary and based on informed consent, with strict confidentiality protocols in place. 

Comparative Value of the Case Cities
The three selected cities represent distinct contexts for understanding gendered mobility. Hong Kong operates a high-capacity metro system central to daily urban mobility but has paid limited institutional attention to women’s specific needs. Delhi has implemented women-only coaches, yet persistent harassment underscores the limits of such interventions. Istanbul, as a transcontinental city bridging Asia and Europe, provides a distinctive cultural and institutional contrast. Together, these cases offer analytical contrasts that illuminate how institutional practices and cultural contexts shape women’s experiences in metro systems. Recruitment will be supported by established collaborators (see section [2d iii] on Project Team and Plan of Collaboration).

Task 4.2 Human-Led Focus Groups with LLM Assistance
To enhance efficiency and comparability across sites, LLM tools will be incorporated in supportive roles during and after focus group research. Specifically, LLMs will assist with transcription, real-time translation, summarization, and thematic coding, enabling multilingual discussions and improving consistency in documentation across locations. Human researchers—the PI and Co-Is—will lead all focus groups, ensuring cultural sensitivity, ethical safeguards, and nuanced engagement with participants. LLMs will serve as assistive tools that reduce the resource burden, minimize moderator variability, and facilitate standardized cross-city comparison. This hybrid model balances the scalability and consistency of AI tools with the adaptive and ethical oversight of human facilitation, ensuring that the focus groups provide both rigorous validation of social media insights and rich contextual interpretation of women’s lived mobility experiences. Discussions will be guided by structured themes to ensure comparability across sites: (1) daily travel and metro use; (2) perceptions of service quality (safety, comfort, dignity, respect); (3) gendered experiences such as harassment, stigma, and underreporting; (4) impacts on travel choices and adaptations; (5) links to employment, education, and social opportunities; (6) social media and experience sharing; and (7) recommendations for service improvements.

Task 5: Prototype Dashboard for Gendered Mobility in Metro Systems
We will develop a prototype for an integrated dashboard architecture that combines advanced visualization capabilities with robust data processing to display gendered metro service quality perceptions derived from social media analytics and enriched by focus group insights. The dashboard will provide policymakers and transit operators with a comprehensive tool to: (1) identify spatial and temporal hotspots of gendered mobility concerns; (2) monitor changes over time and the effectiveness of interventions; and (3) compare patterns across cities and cultural contexts.

The prototype will feature a modular design with panels for spatial analysis, temporal patterns, service quality dimensions, and gender-specific sentiment distributions. Interactive functions will include heatmaps for spatial concentrations of concerns, time-series charts for temporal variations, and comparative visualizations highlighting gender disparities. GIS integration will enable precise mapping of sentiment hotspots to physical metro infrastructure, with drill-down functionality from system-level views to individual stations. Figure A3 in the Appendix illustrates the prototype.

At the conclusion of the project, this prototype will serve as a proof-of-concept. Building on it, we plan to apply for the Innovation and Technology Fund and other research grants to transform the prototype into a full-fledged operational dashboard, ensuring sustainability and long-term adoption by metro operators and government agencies.
