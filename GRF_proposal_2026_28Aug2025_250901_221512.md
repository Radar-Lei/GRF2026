Title: 
Gender and Transport Equity in Asia: A Comparative Study of Women’s Mobility in Metro Transit through Social Media Analytics



Principal Investigator: Sylvia He
The Chinese University of Hong Kong



Keywords: Transport Equity, Metro Transit, Gendered Mobility, Social Media Analytics, Large Language Models 
 
Abstract (max. 400 words)
Urbanization has significantly improved transport infrastructure worldwide, yet our transport systems may not serve all population groups equally. Women face distinct mobility challenges including voyeurism, sexual harassment, and disrespect for breastfeeding—issues that remain largely invisible due to social norms, cultural pressures, and fear of stigma. This problem is particularly acute in Asia, which has witnessed some of the most notorious cases of gendered sexual assault and violence in public transport, presenting a distinct gendered experience of public transport mobility in Asian cities. We focus on Asia for three reasons: (1) the region has the world’s fastest-growing metro systems and highest metro ridership share, so gender equity affect hundreds of millions of daily trips; (2) despite frequent media reports, systematic evidence on gendered harassment is sparse due to cultural stigma and under-reporting; and (3) Asia’s diversity in income, gender norms, and digital adoption enables comparative analysis with findings transferable to other rapidly urbanizing regions.

Traditional research methods such as questionnaire surveys have provided valuable insights into women’s mobility experiences, but they face limitations in scale, real-time data capture, and the ability to uncover unspoken realities across diverse cultural contexts. These methods often suffer from sampling biases and limited geographical coverage, particularly in developing countries. Moreover, traditional approaches struggle to capture the spontaneous nature of gendered experiences that often go unreported. While social media analytics offers better scale and real-time insights, it faces representativeness challenges that could bias findings toward digitally active populations. Social media users are typically younger and more educated compared to the general population, creating biases that underrepresent vulnerable groups. 

This research addresses these limitations through an innovative mixed-methods approach that combines social media analytics with LLM-assisted focus group studies in three tentatively selected cities to capture women’s mobility experiences in metro systems. We propose a comprehensive framework that: (1) leverages social media data mining to uncover gendered experiences across diverse contexts while acknowledging digital population biases; (2) develops customized large language models for accurate sentiment analysis and interpretation of gendered mobility barriers; and (3) implements LLM-assisted focus group studies that enhance data collection efficiency through AI moderation while maintaining cultural sensitivity through human oversight. The study will generate a unique dataset of gendered metro experiences across Asian contexts, provide actionable insights for policymakers and transit operators, and contribute to creating safer, more equitable urban environments that empower women's mobility and access to opportunities.
 
Research Details 

1.	Objectives and Pathways to Impact Statement
a)	Project Objectives (max. 800 words)
Achieving transport equity requires addressing the needs of socially disadvantaged groups, including women. The transport equity literature has shown that different social strata benefit unevenly from transit services. Women, in particular, often face distinct mobility challenges compared with men. Many of these challenges—such as voyeurism, sexual harassment, and the disrespect of breastfeeding—are underreported due to social norms, cultural pressures, and fear of stigma. As a result, they remain largely invisible. A safer and more inclusive travel experience can increase women’s mobility, empowering them to access employment, education, and social opportunities across the city and beyond. From a societal perspective, improving women’s experiences in public transit not only enhances their well-being but also supports broader goals of social inclusion and gender equality. In Hong Kong, urban metros are the backbone of the transport network, carrying the majority of daily trips. Yet even in these systems, women may not always feel safe, particularly in rush hours where the metro compartments are overcrowded. In contrast, in cities where metros are less dominant and public transport use is lower, reduced crowds do not necessarily translate into safety. Limited public surveillance can increase women’s vulnerability to harassment and other harmful acts. This issue is particularly acute in developing countries, where limited surveillance and policing exacerbate the problem. To address this gap, the proposed research will use a social media analytics approach to capture and analyze unspoken, real-world experiences of women in metro systems. Theoretically, it contributes to the transport equity discourse by foregrounding gendered mobility barriers. Methodologically, it introduces a novel application of social media analytics in transport research. Empirically, it will build a unique dataset of gendered metro experiences across diverse contexts. Policy and practice wise, the study will provide actionable insights for policymakers and transit operators to improve safety, inclusivity, and equity in metro systems.

Objective 1: To select a list of cities and to collect data on passengers’ perception of metro systems

For city selection for case studies, we will employ a comprehensive set of indicators that capture economic development, gender equality, urban characteristics, and digital infrastructure. Beyond GDP per capita and the Gender Inequality Index (GII), our selection framework includes: (1) internet penetration rates to evaluate digital accessibility; (2) urban development indicators including population density and service sector employment; (3) transport-specific metrics such as metro usage patterns; and (5) additional gender-specific indicators including educational attainment gaps and labor force participation differentials.

Objective 2: To develop user profiling techniques for identifying gendered experiences and extracting authentic metro-related social media data

The second objective focuses on developing advanced user profiling and demographic inference techniques to identify female metro users and extract authentic gendered travel experiences from social media data. Building on demographic inference methods, we will create a multi-modal framework that integrates name-based matching, linguistic analysis, social network propagation, and profile image analysis to infer user gender and demographic attributes across diverse cultural contexts.

Objective 3: To develop a large language model for sentiment analysis of gendered experiences on the metro systems

The third objective focuses on developing a customized LLM specifically designed for sentiment analysis of gendered experiences in metro systems. We will create a specialized LLM architecture that integrates multilingual processing capabilities with domain-specific knowledge about transit operations and gendered mobility patterns. This model will be trained on carefully curated social media data from the selected cities, incorporating both textual content and associated spatio-temporal metadata to enable context-aware sentiment classification.

Objective 4: To implement LLM-assisted focus group studies for mechanism elicitation and contextual interpretation

We will implement LLM-assisted focus group studies to deepen interpretation, surface mechanisms, and co-produce culturally grounded explanations of social media patterns. Through purposive stratified sampling across salient user segments, focus groups will elicit collective sense-making, uncover tacit norms and coping strategies, and provide rich contextualization of quantitative signals. The hybrid approach combines AI moderation with human oversight to enhance data collection efficiency while maintaining cultural sensitivity, employing Speech-to-Text and Text-to-Speech systems optimized for multi-participant discussions across language barriers.

Objective 5: To develop an integrated dashboard for visualizing gendered metro experiences    

An integrated platform will be developed to visualize spatio-temporal patterns of gendered service quality perceptions while incorporating qualitative insights from direct engagement with women metro users: the dashboard will feature interactive functions for displaying sentiment scores, temporal variations (hourly, daily, seasonal), and gender-specific sentiment distributions, using heatmaps for spatial traveling concern concentrations and comparative visualizations to highlight gender disparities.

Research Project Statement (max. 7 pages)

i.	Research Context 

 (a) Gendered Mobility Barriers in Metro Systems
Women face distinct mobility challenges in public transit systems worldwide, with research consistently demonstrating pervasive yet underreported gendered barriers due to social norms and fear of stigma (Mazumder & Pokharel, 2018; King et al., 2021). These challenges encompass profound safety concerns, with women reporting significantly higher levels of fear and insecurity compared to men in metro environments (Ouali et al., 2020; Ceccato et al., 2024). Historical and socio-cultural factors deeply influence women's mobility patterns. Panjwani (2018) reveals how socio-cultural norms continue to shape contemporary mobility restrictions in developing contexts, while Field and Vyborny (2022) demonstrate that safety and social acceptability constitute primary barriers to women's mobility, highlighting the importance of gender-sensitive transport services.

The manifestations of gendered barriers vary across contexts but share common themes of harassment and restricted access. From sexual violence in Bangladesh (Mazumder & Pokharel, 2018) to heightened insecurity in Latin America (Infante-Vargas & Boyer, 2021; Joshi et al., 2022), women are compelled to restrict their mobility and prioritize safety through extensive behavioral adaptations. Beyond safety concerns, women face significant time poverty exacerbated by entrenched gender roles and caregiving responsibilities (Hail & McQuaid, 2021). This time poverty, combined with the challenges of accessing equitable transport services (Pourhashem et al., 2022), creates complex mobility constraints of women that traditional research methods often fail to capture comprehensively.

Our systematic review indicates a predominant reliance on single-method designs that constrain validity, coverage, and transferability of findings. Many studies rely either on cross-sectional questionnaires that are vulnerable to recall and social desirability biases (Kerzhner et al., 2018) and offer limited spatio-temporal coverage (Ceccato et al., 2024), or on stand-alone qualitative interviews/focus groups that provide rich narratives yet make it difficult to assess prevalence, support cross-city comparison, and link mechanisms to patterns at scale (Sil et al., 2023). Single-method approaches also struggle to surface underreported and culturally sensitive experiences—particularly salient in Asian contexts where stigma suppresses disclosure and they lack triangulation across independent data sources. Addressing this limitation calls for a mixed-methods strategy that integrates large-scale quantitative evidence (e.g., digital trace and survey data) with qualitative insights (e.g., culturally sensitive focus groups) to achieve both breadth and depth, enable cross-context comparisons, and enhance the credibility and actionability of evidence on gendered mobility barriers in metro systems.

(b) Methodological Innovations in Social Media Analytics
The limitations of traditional methods in capturing underreported gendered experiences have prompted innovative approaches using social media analytics (SMA). SMA offers near real-time insights into public perceptions, complementing traditional survey and qualitative methods (Thelwall, 2017; Yigitcanlar et al., 2020). Importantly, social media platforms serve as vital sites for the circulation of public discourse about sexual harassment and gender-based violence in public transport, providing spaces where women can share experiences that might otherwise remain silenced. As one of our Co-Is, Tan (2023) demonstrates in her analysis of feminist digital activism in China, social media platforms enable creative feminist strategies to emerge for addressing sexual assault and harassment in public transit, raising awareness, and building solidarity among women facing similar challenges. In transport research, SMA has demonstrated significant potential for uncovering service quality issues. Luo and He (2020, 2021) pioneered social media mining for transit services, revealing temporal and spatial variations in perceptions while identifying distinct gender gaps in concerns—with women focusing more on comfort and safety. The methodological landscape is rapidly evolving with advanced computational techniques. Deep learning approaches and sentiment analysis frameworks (Collins et al., 2013; El-Diraby et al., 2019) have enhanced processing power and predictive accuracy for complex text analysis tasks in transportation contexts. The emergence of Large Language Models represents a transformative advancement. Specialized LLMs fine-tuned on domain-specific data demonstrate potential for understanding nuanced expressions in specific contexts, with multimodal capabilities enhancing analysis of visual and textual information interplay (Li et al., 2023; Tian et al., 2020).
 
(c) User Profiling Challenge
A critical methodological challenge in gendered mobility research is accurate demographic identification, particularly gender attributes from social media data. Traditional approaches relying on self-reported gender or platform data face limitations from privacy concerns, incomplete profiles, and cultural variations in gender expression, leading to a fundamental barrier to distinguishing authentic gendered experiences from general transit feedback (Liu et al., 2023). Recent advances in multi-modal demographic inference offer promising solutions. Techniques combining name-based prediction, linguistic style analysis, social network propagation, and profile image analysis demonstrate improved accuracy in cross-cultural gender attributes (Wang & Zhang, 2022). The integration of advanced user profiling with sentiment analysis represents a novel contribution to transport equity research. By developing robust frameworks for identifying gendered experiences, this research addresses a critical methodological gap enabling comparative analysis of women's mobility barriers across diverse urban and cultural contexts.

ii.	Research Questions 
This study addresses the following five research questions:

Q1: How can we select Asian cities for case studies and effectively collect social media data to enable comparative analysis of gendered mobility experiences?
This question will be addressed through Task 1, which establishes a comprehensive multidimensional framework for selecting diverse global cities based on GDP per capita, Gender Inequality Index, digital infrastructure, and urban characteristics. The task develops robust infrastructure for multilingual social media data collection and preprocessing, ensuring representative coverage across different development levels and gender equality contexts to enable systematic comparative analysis.

Q2: How can we accurately identify female metro users and extract authentic gendered travel experiences from multilingual social media data across diverse cultural contexts?
Task 2 directly addresses this question by developing comprehensive frameworks that integrate name-based matching, linguistic analysis, social network analysis, and profile image analysis for demographic inference. The task creates specialized filtering techniques to distinguish authentic gendered metro experiences from incidental mentions, with rigorous validation procedures to assess accuracy across different cultural contexts while maintaining ethical guidelines for handling sensitive demographic information.

Q3: To what extent can LLMs accurately identify and classify expressions of gendered mobility barriers in multilingual social media data with spatio-temporal traffic context integration? 
This question is addressed through Task 3, which creates specialized LLM architectures optimized for gendered sentiment analysis in transit contexts. The task develops domain-specific sentiment analysis capabilities with multilingual processing, spatio-temporal context integration, and comprehensive validation frameworks to assess model performance across linguistic, cultural, and operational contexts, overcoming limitations of generic sentiment analysis tools.

Q4: How can we use LLMs to help focus group studies effectively validate social media analytics findings? 
Task 4 will directly tackle this question through strategic focus groups in three cities (Hong Kong, Delhi, and Istanbul) designed to deepen interpretation and interrogate mechanisms via purposive sampling across salient user segments. The task implements an innovative hybrid approach combining AI moderation with human oversight, providing three qualitative contributions: pattern interpretation, mechanism discovery, and contextualization to ensure robust triangulation and theory building.

Q5: How can an integrated dashboard system effectively visualize and analyze gendered mobility patterns from combined social media analytics and focus group insights?
This question will be addressed through Task 5, which develops comprehensive visualization platforms that integrate sentiment analysis outputs with real-time operational data. The task creates advanced analytical features, including spatial-temporal mapping, trend analysis, and comparative benchmarking across cities, enabling policymakers to identify spatial and temporal hotspots of gendered mobility concerns and monitor intervention effectiveness while accounting for digital divide effects.

Task 1: City Selection and Social Media Data Collection
Task 1.1  Selection of Global Cities
We will employ a comprehensive multidimensional framework for selecting cities with metro systems that represent diverse economic, social, and digital contexts. The selection criteria encompass five key dimensions: economic development (GDP per capita), gender equality (Gender Inequality Index), digital infrastructure (internet penetration rates), urban characteristics (population density, service sector employment), and transport-specific metrics (metro usage patterns). This approach ensures representation across different development levels and gender equality contexts, enabling comparative analysis of gendered mobility barriers across varied socioeconomic environments. We will employ a multidimensional framework for selecting cities with metro systems that represent diverse economic, social, and digital contexts.

Our city selection methodology employs a systematic filtering process from a comprehensive database of 186 global metro cities. We first collect data across five dimensions: GDP per capita (World Bank 2023), Gender Inequality Index (UNDP 2023), internet penetration rates (World Bank/ITU 2023), urban characteristics (population density and service sector employment from Our World in Data and ILO 2022-2023), and metro-specific metrics (ridership patterns and operational characteristics from metro operators). Primary filtering criteria include internet penetration ≥60%, metro operation ≥5 years, daily ridership ≥100,000 passengers, and availability of gender-disaggregated transport data to ensure research feasibility and adequate social media content generation. From the filtered pool, cities are categorized into six analytical quadrants based on indicator combinations to ensure representative coverage across different development and gender equality contexts. Final selection balances analytical requirements with practical considerations including social media content volume (minimum 10,000 annual metro-related posts), linguistic diversity, geographic distribution, and operational accessibility for research partnerships.

This multidimensional approach ensures comprehensive representation across different development levels and gender equality contexts, enabling robust comparative analysis of gendered mobility barriers across varied socioeconomic environments. The framework is designed to capture the complex interplay between economic conditions, social structures, digital accessibility, urban form, and transit system attributes that collectively shape women's metro experiences. Preliminary analysis (see Figure A2) demonstrates the distribution of global cities with metro systems across GDP and GII quadrants, revealing opportunities for comparative analysis across different development levels and gender equality contexts.

Task 1.2 Social Media Data Collection and Preprocessing
Social media data will be systematically collected from major platforms, such as Twitter, Facebook, and Instagram. Using keywords related to metro systems in multiple languages, we will retrieve user-generated content spanning a 3-year period (from 2022 to 2024) with geolocation filtering, temporal parameters, and metadata extraction. The protocol ensures comprehensive coverage of gendered experiences while maintaining ethical data handling practices and compliance with platform terms of service. Collected data will undergo a series of preprocessing including deduplication, language identification, spam/bot removal, and anonymization to protect user privacy. A structured database will be created with standardized fields for text content, metadata, sentiment labels, and gender indicators. The preprocessing pipeline incorporates automated quality checks and manual validation to maintain data integrity throughout the collection and management process.

We will develop a robust infrastructure for processing multilingual social media content, building upon our preliminary work that has demonstrated 78.1% F1 score in identifying transit-related posts from raw social media data and 80.8% F1 score in sentiment analysis accuracy. Our approach will leverage state-of-the-art natural language processing techniques including: (1) hybrid LLM-based filtering using mixture-of-experts architecture with instruction refinement to accurately distinguish genuine passenger experiences from incidental mentions; (2) fine-tuned RoBERTa models with spatio-temporal encoders for context-aware sentiment classification; (3) multilingual processing capabilities with language-specific tokenization (multilingual Qwen embedding model) and culturally-sensitive text normalization; (4) advanced geoparsing techniques achieving 87.6% accuracy in location extraction with topological constraints; and (5) temporal encoding for peak-hour pattern recognition. The infrastructure will support real-time data ingestion, batch processing capabilities, and scalable storage solutions to handle large volumes of social media data from diverse global contexts, ensuring efficient processing across different operational requirements.

Task 2: User Profiling and Demographic Inference for Gendered Experience Identification
Task 2.1 Multi-modal Demographic Inference Framework Development
We will develop a comprehensive multi-modal framework for demographic inference and user profiling, specifically designed to identify female metro users and extract authentic gendered travel experiences from social media data. The framework will integrate four complementary approaches: (1) name-based matching using culturally-specific name dictionaries to infer gender and ethnicity across different cultural contexts (Wang & Zhang, 2022); (2) linguistic analysis of user posts and self-descriptions to identify gender-associated language patterns and expressions; (3) social network analysis using graph propagation techniques (Label Propagation and Graph Convolutional Networks) to infer demographic attributes based on homophily patterns in followership networks; and (4) profile image analysis using computer vision techniques to extract gender-related visual features (Liang et al., 2018).

The name-based matching component will leverage name dictionaries for each target region, incorporating local naming conventions and addressing challenges of name ambiguity and cultural specificity (Lasri et al., 2023). For linguistic analysis, we will develop language models that can identify gender-associated linguistic patterns while accounting for cultural variations in expression (Chen et al., 2015). The graph-based propagation methods will enable robust demographic inference by leveraging network connectivity to propagate information from labeled to unlabeled users (Wang et al., 2019). Profile analysis will employ state-of-the-art computer vision models trained on diverse demographic datasets.

Task 2.2 Metro-Specific Content Filtering from raw social media data
Building upon the demographic inference framework, we will develop specialized filtering techniques to distinguish authentic gendered metro experiences from incidental mentions and unrelated content. This will involve creating a hybrid filtering mechanism that combines LLM-driven semantic relevance scoring with domain-specific knowledge about transit contexts (Goodchild, 2007; Schweitzer, 2014). The filtering process will employ instruction-guided optimization to iteratively refine the identification of genuine passenger feedback, ensuring that only posts reflecting actual travel experiences are included in the analysis.

Validation procedures will assess the accuracy of demographic inference across different cultural contexts using metrics for gender classification accuracy, coverage rates, and cross-cultural consistency. We will implement rigorous quality assurance measures including manual validation of inferred demographic attributes, cross-validation with external demographic data sources, and sensitivity study to evaluate the robustness of inference methods across different user populations. Ethical guidelines will address potential biases in demographic inference, with special attention to ensuring fair performance across different demographic groups and cultural contexts, while maintaining strict privacy protection protocols for handling sensitive demographic information.

Task 3: Development of Customized Large Language Models for Sentiment Analysis
Task 3.1 LLM Architecture Development and Training Data Curation
A customized large language model is essential because generic sentiment analysis tools fail to capture the nuanced expressions of gendered mobility experiences in metro systems. Off-the-shelf models lack domain-specific understanding of transit operations, safety concerns, and culturally specific expressions of harassment and discomfort (Luo & He, 2021; Rashidi et al., 2017). They struggle with indirect expressions, multilingual contexts, and spatio-temporal variations that characterize women’s metro experiences globally.

The customized LLM will perform specialized functions including: (1) domain-specific sentiment analysis fine-tuned for transit vocabulary and gendered experience indicators, building on state-of-the-art approaches like RoBERTa (Liu et al., 2019) and SKEP (Tian et al., 2020); (2) multilingual processing capabilities for diverse cultural contexts, leveraging techniques demonstrated in multilingual sentiment analysis (Grant-Muller et al., 2014); (3) spatio-temporal context integration to associate sentiments with specific operational conditions; (4) recognition of indirect and subtle expressions of gendered experiences; and (5) bias mitigation strategies for equitable performance across demographic groups.

Expected deliverables include a novel Transformer-based architecture optimized for gendered sentiment analysis, a curated multilingual training dataset of annotated social media posts, fine-tuned model weights ready for deployment, comprehensive validation frameworks, and complete technical documentation. This customized approach will overcome limitations of generic tools by providing accurate, context-aware sentiment analysis tailored to gendered mobility barriers, following the methodological advancements in domain-specific LLM fine-tuning demonstrated by Li et al. (2023) and Tian et al. (2020).

Task 3.2 Context Integration and Model Validation Framework
The customized LLM will incorporate spatio-temporal encoders to enable context-aware sentiment analysis, learning to associate sentiment expressions with specific operational contexts including time of day, station characteristics, route configurations, and seasonal variations. The model will be trained to recognize how safety perceptions and gendered experiences vary across different global cities, providing nuanced understanding of metro system dynamics and user experiences.

Validation procedures will assess model performance across linguistic, cultural, and operational contexts using metrics for sentiment classification accuracy, cross-cultural consistency, and contextual understanding. Comparative analysis against generic sentiment analysis tools will demonstrate superior performance in capturing gendered experiences, with validation including both quantitative metrics and qualitative assessments by domain experts to ensure practical utility and cultural sensitivity.

Ethical guidelines will be established for LLM development and deployment, addressing potential biases in sentiment analysis across demographic groups and cultural contexts. We will implement bias detection and mitigation strategies to ensure fair and equitable performance, with regular audits for cultural sensitivity, privacy protection, and ethical considerations in handling sensitive content related to harassment and safety concerns, ensuring responsible AI development practices.

Task 4: Focus Group Studies with LLM Assistance
Task 4.1 Enhancing Social Media Analytics through Strategic Focus Groups
While social media analytics offers unprecedented scale and real-time signals, qualitative focus groups contribute complementary strengths: they leverage group interaction to co-construct meaning, surface tacit norms and sensitive experiences, generate new insights through participant-to-participant dialogue, and allow observation of nonverbal cues and consensus/dissent dynamics—advantages well documented in the qualitative methods literature (Kitzinger, 1995; Morgan, 1996; Krueger & Casey, 2015; Nyumba et al., 2018).

Three cities—Hong Kong, Delhi, and Istanbul—will host the focus groups. Each session has three goals: (1) pattern interpretation—use clear prompts to interpret patterns found in social media analytics (SMA); (2) mechanism exploration—probe why barriers arise and how women adapt; (3) contextualization—surface cultural and institutional factors that shape mobility. This mixed-methods design prioritizes depth, explanation, and triangulation over statistical representativeness, producing credible and actionable insights.

Task 4.2 LLM-Assisted Focus Group Implementation
Building on advances in LLM-powered focus group facilitation (Zhang et al., 2024), we will implement an innovative hybrid approach combining AI moderation with human oversight to enhance data collection efficiency while maintaining cultural sensitivity. LLM-assisted focus groups offer significant advantages over traditional approaches: (1) Reducing resource and labor requirements in traditional focus group studies which often require skilled human moderator; (2) Improving consistency and standardization across cultural contexts and locations, reducing moderator variability that can affect data quality; and (3) Multilingual capabilities enabling discussions across language barriers through translation support.

The AI moderator will employ a reflection mechanism to maintain discussion coherence and prevent memory loss during extended sessions, incorporating voice-based interaction capabilities through Speech-to-Text and Text-to-Speech systems optimized for multi-participant discussions. To address limitations identified in LLM-human interaction (Stafford et al., 2024), human co-facilitators will provide oversight for cultural nuance interpretation, handle complex emotional responses, and ensure authentic participant engagement. This hybrid model combines the scalability and consistency of AI moderation with the cultural sensitivity and adaptive capabilities of human facilitation, enabling more efficient data collection across multiple cities while maintaining research quality and ethical standards for sensitive discussions about gendered mobility experiences.

Task 5: Develop a Prototype for an Integrated Dashboard Development for Gendered Mobility Analytics
We will develop a prototype for an integrated dashboard architecture that combines advanced visualization capabilities with robust data processing to display gendered metro service quality perceptions derived from social media analytics and enhanced by focus group insights. The dashboard will incorporate insights from both social media analytics and focus group studies to provide a comprehensive visualization platform that enables policymakers and transit operators to: (1) Identify spatial and temporal hotspots of gendered mobility concerns across metro systems; (2) Monitor the effectiveness of interventions and policy implementations over time; (3) Compare gendered mobility patterns across different cities and cultural contexts. The dashboard features a modular design with separate panels for spatial analysis, temporal patterns, service quality dimensions, and gender-specific sentiment distributions, employing a responsive web framework for interactive visualizations and spatio-temporal mapping. Advanced visualization techniques include heatmaps for spatial concentrations of gendered traveling concerns, time-series charts for temporal variations, and comparative visualizations highlighting gender disparities, with GIS integration enabling precise mapping of sentiment hotspots to physical metro infrastructure. Figure A3 in Appendix shows an illustration of the prototype of the dashboard. Advanced analytical features include trend analysis tools, anomaly detection algorithms, and comparative benchmarking across cities, supporting drill-down functionality from aggregate system views to individual station-level analyses. At the end of this project, we plan to apply the Innovation and Technology Fund and other research funds to develop a full-fledge dashboard.
