This project directly supports the Innovation and Technology Fund's (ITF) mandate to enhance competitiveness and promote innovation that contributes to the long-term economic and social development of Hong Kong.1The Innovation: 'Equi-Mobility HK' is a cloud-based, AI-driven policy simulation tool designed for public sector transport planners. The platform integrates disparate data sources—including anonymised Octopus card data, census information, GIS mapping, and qualitative findings from the foundational 'Gender and Transport Equity in Asia' research—to model, predict, and visualise the gender-differentiated impacts of transport policy and infrastructure changes. This foundational investment will catalyse the development of a technology with the potential to deliver significant, lasting benefits to the people of Hong Kong and establish a new global standard for inclusive urban mobility.Section 2: From Research to Reality: The 'Equi-Mobility HK' Analytics PlatformThe foundational research on 'Gender and Transport Equity in Asia' reveals a critical gap in urban planning: traditional methodologies, which rely on aggregate data, consistently fail to capture the nuanced and often disparate mobility needs, travel patterns, and safety concerns of different genders. This oversight leads to the development of transport systems that are less efficient, less safe, and less accessible for a significant portion of the population, resulting in suboptimal infrastructure investments and reinforcing social inequities. 'Equi-Mobility HK' is the proposed technological solution designed to bridge this gap, transforming qualitative social insights into a quantitative, predictive, and actionable tool for policymakers.The Solution - 'Equi-Mobility HK''Equi-Mobility HK' is conceived not merely as a data visualisation tool but as a sophisticated decision-support system. Its value lies in its ability to empower transport planners to move beyond reactive problem-solving and embrace a proactive, evidence-based approach that centres on human experience. By simulating the potential equity impacts of decisions before capital is committed, the platform introduces a new layer of financial prudence and risk management into public sector planning. This dual benefit—achieving both social good and fiscal responsibility—presents a compelling value proposition for government bodies.Data Fusion EngineThe technical core of the platform is its Data Fusion Engine. This component is designed to ingest, clean, and harmonise a wide array of data types. On the quantitative side, it will process large-scale datasets such as anonymised Octopus card transaction records, public transport GPS data, census demographics, and GIS layers detailing infrastructure like lighting, footpaths, and CCTV coverage. Simultaneously, it will incorporate qualitative data derived from the foundational research, including findings from focus groups, survey responses on perceived safety, and ethnographic studies on travel behaviour. The engine will use advanced data structuring and tagging techniques to convert these qualitative insights into machine-readable formats that can be correlated with the quantitative data streams.AI-Powered Simulation CoreThe platform's predictive power resides in its AI-Powered Simulation Core. This module will employ a suite of machine learning models to analyse the fused dataset and identify hidden correlations between transport infrastructure, service provision, and gender-differentiated outcomes. For example, the models can learn the relationship between station lighting levels, time of day, and reported feelings of safety among female travellers.Once trained, this core enables the platform's primary function: scenario simulation. A planner could propose a change, such as altering a bus route or reducing the frequency of late-night MTR services, and the AI core would predict the likely consequences across various gender-specific metrics. It would answer critical questions like: "What is the projected increase in commute time for women who rely on this service for care-related trips?" or "How will the closure of a specific MTR exit after 10 pm affect the perceived safety of female commuters in that district?"Equity Impact DashboardThe outputs of the simulation core are presented through an intuitive, user-friendly Equity Impact Dashboard. This interface is designed for non-technical users, such as policy advisors and transport planners. Key features will include:GIS-Based Visualisations: Interactive maps of Hong Kong displaying transport networks overlaid with data visualisations, such as heat maps showing areas of low perceived safety or routes with high accessibility to essential services like childcare centres and healthcare facilities.Scenario Comparison: A tool allowing users to compare the predicted outcomes of multiple policy scenarios side-by-side.Equity Scorecard: A quantitative dashboard that scores proposed transport changes against a set of predefined Key Performance Indicators (KPIs). These KPIs will be co-developed with community stakeholders and will include metrics such as travel time parity, cost burden, accessibility to employment and services, and a composite safety score.Intellectual Property (IP)The primary intellectual property generated by this project will be the proprietary algorithms within the Data Fusion Engine and the AI-Powered Simulation Core. Specifically, the novel methods for quantifying and integrating qualitative safety and accessibility data with quantitative transport metrics, along with the predictive models that constitute the simulation engine, will form a unique and defensible IP asset. This aligns directly with the ITF's objective of supporting R&D that generates valuable intellectual property, which can later be commercialised, as envisioned in Phase 3 of this plan.6Section 3: Strategic Alignment with Hong Kong's Smart City and Social Innovation GoalsThe 'Equi-Mobility HK' project is not a standalone academic exercise; it is strategically designed to align with and actively contribute to the HKSAR Government's highest-level policy objectives in innovation, technology, and social development. By framing the project within this broader strategic context, its value proposition to the Innovation and Technology Commission (ITC) is significantly amplified. It is presented not merely as a transport solution, but as a catalyst for progress across multiple government priorities, representing a highly efficient and impactful use of public funds.Contribution to "Smart City Blueprint for Hong Kong"The project directly supports the vision outlined in the "Smart City Blueprint for Hong Kong." It makes tangible contributions to at least two of the blueprint's six major pillars:Smart Mobility: The platform moves beyond generic efficiency gains to deliver inclusive efficiency. By optimising transport networks for the specific travel patterns of all users, including trip-chaining for caregiving duties which are disproportionately performed by women, 'Equi-Mobility HK' helps create a transport system that is truly smarter and more responsive to the diverse needs of the populace.Smart Living: A core objective of the project is to enhance public safety and create a more inclusive urban environment. By enabling planners to identify and mitigate safety risks in the transport network—such as poorly lit walkways or isolated bus stops—the platform directly contributes to a higher quality of life and a greater sense of security for all citizens, a key component of "Smart Living."Enhancing Data-Driven GovernanceThe Innovation, Technology and Industry Bureau (ITIB) has identified the facilitation of "big data applications" as a key work priority to strengthen governance.10 'Equi-Mobility HK' serves as a premier example of this principle in action. It demonstrates how government-held and publicly available data can be fused with academic research and community input to formulate more effective, equitable, and evidence-based public policy. The platform provides a practical tool for government departments to harness the power of data, moving from a descriptive "what is happening" model to a predictive "what will happen if" model of governance.Fostering Social InnovationThe project embodies the principles of social innovation by applying cutting-edge technology to address a pressing societal challenge. This dual focus aligns perfectly with the ethos of specific ITF schemes, most notably the Innovation and Technology Fund for Better Living (FBL). The FBL was established with $500 million to finance precisely these types of projects: those that use innovation and technology to "make people's daily living more convenient, comfortable and safer, or address the needs of specific community groups".2 The project's focus on improving transport for women, the elderly, and other vulnerable groups makes it an ideal candidate for FBL support.Positioning Hong Kong as a Global LeaderBy tackling the globally relevant urban challenge of transport equity, this project has the potential to generate exportable technology and expertise. As cities worldwide grapple with making their transport systems more inclusive, a validated and commercialised 'Equi-Mobility HK' platform could be marketed to other metropolitan areas in Asia and beyond. This would not only create economic value but also enhance Hong Kong's international reputation as a hub for "CivicTech"—technology designed for public benefit—complementing its established strengths in finance and trade. This contributes to the ITF's overarching goal of increasing the competitiveness of Hong Kong's economic activities.12Section 4: A Phased Funding Strategy Through the Innovation and Technology FundA sophisticated understanding of the Innovation and Technology Fund (ITF) ecosystem reveals that a single funding application is suboptimal for a project of this nature. Instead, a multi-year, multi-scheme strategy is proposed. This phased approach is strategically superior as it mitigates risk for both the project team and the ITC, allows for iterative development based on real-world feedback, and systematically builds a track record of successful execution. Most importantly, it aligns the project's evolving needs—from non-commercial R&D to public sector validation and finally to commercial scaling—with the specific mandates of different ITF schemes, maximising the probability of sustained support.Phase 1: Prototyping & Community Engagement (Months 1-12)Primary Scheme: Innovation and Technology Fund for Better Living (FBL) 2Justification: The FBL is the ideal entry point for 'Equi-Mobility HK'. Its explicit focus is on non-profit-making I&T projects that deliver clear social benefits, with transportation being a designated theme.2 The project's initial phase is purely developmental and aimed at improving public life, fitting the FBL's criteria perfectly. The scheme's generous terms—funding up to 90% of eligible costs with a ceiling of $5 million 2—provide the substantial capital required to develop a robust Minimum Viable Product (MVP) without the premature pressure of securing private investment or defining a commercial model.Key Activities: The core activities in this phase will be to develop the Data Fusion Engine and the initial AI simulation models. A significant portion of the budget will be allocated to partnering with eligible FBL applicants, such as NGOs and social service organisations 2, to conduct workshops, gather essential qualitative data, and co-design the platform's user interface and features to ensure they meet genuine community needs.Talent Scheme: Research Talent Hub (RTH) 13Justification: Concurrently with the FBL application, an application will be made to the RTH. This demonstrates a highly efficient and leveraged funding model to the ITC.Phase 2: Public Sector Validation & Trial (Months 13-24)Primary Scheme: Public Sector Trial Scheme – ITF Projects (PSTS-ITF) 4Justification: Upon successful completion of Phase 1, the project will possess a tangible R&D outcome (the MVP), making it eligible for the PSTS-ITF.5 This scheme is the critical bridge between the laboratory and the real world, designed specifically to fund the production of prototypes and the execution of trials within the public sector to promote commercialisation.9 It is the logical next step to validate the platform's utility and impact.Key Activities: The primary objective of this phase is to secure a trial partnership and deploy the 'Equi-Mobility HK' platform in a real-world planning context. The team will proactively engage the PSTS Helpdesk, an ITC resource designed to match applicants with suitable government departments and public bodies.5 A trial with the Transport Department or MTR Corporation to analyse an upcoming route change or infrastructure project would provide an invaluable case study, generating critical validation data and a powerful testimonial for future commercialisation efforts.Talent Scheme: The core team will continue to be funded via the RTH, ensuring continuity of technical expertise throughout the validation phase.Phase 3: Commercialization & Scaling (Months 25-36+)Primary Scheme: Technology Start-up Support Scheme for Universities (TSSSU) 6Justification: With a prototype developed under the FBL and validated through a successful PSTS trial, the project will be perfectly positioned for commercial spin-off. The Pathway to Impact: Technology Transfer and Commercialization RoadmapA successful technology transfer plan requires more than just innovative technology and a sound funding strategy; it demands a clear and actionable roadmap for moving the intellectual property out of the university and into a sustainable entity that can deliver long-term impact. This section outlines the proposed framework for IP management, stakeholder engagement, and the business model for the 'Equi-Mobility HK' social enterprise.Intellectual Property ManagementThe intellectual property (IP) developed during Phases 1 and 2—specifically the proprietary algorithms and predictive models—will be owned and managed by the lead university's Technology Transfer Office (TTO) or equivalent body.20 This ensures that the IP is protected and handled in accordance with established institutional policies. Upon the formation of the spin-off company in Phase 3, the TTO will execute a formal licensing agreement with the new entity. This agreement will be structured to grant the start-up the necessary rights to commercialise the technology while ensuring a fair return to the university, typically through a combination of equity, royalties, or licensing fees. This approach is standard practice for university spin-offs and is well-supported within the framework of ITF schemes like TSSSU.6Stakeholder Engagement PlanContinuous and strategic engagement with key stakeholders is critical for the project's success at every stage.Public Sector: In Phase 1, initial contact will be made with the Transport Department, MTR Corporation Limited, and the Lands Department to introduce the project and understand their key planning challenges. This relationship will be formalized in Phase 2 through the PSTS trial, transforming them from observers into active partners. In Phase 3, these entities will be targeted as the primary anchor clients for the commercial SaaS platform.Community Sector: The partnerships forged with NGOs and community groups during the FBL-funded Phase 1 will be maintained throughout the project's lifecycle. These organisations are not just data sources; they are crucial validation partners who ensure the platform remains grounded in the lived experiences of the communities it aims to serve. They will be engaged for ongoing feedback, user testing, and advocacy.Private Sector: The commercialization strategy hinges on attracting private investment to unlock the matching funds available under the TSSSU+ scheme.6 The engagement plan for the private sector will be timed strategically. Following the successful completion of the PSTS trial in Phase 2, the project team will have a compelling, de-risked investment proposition: a validated technology with a clear public sector endorsement. The team will target angel investors and venture capital funds with a stated focus on social impact, "GovTech," or smart city technology.Business Model for the Social EnterpriseThe spin-off company will be incorporated as a social enterprise, legally embedding a dual mission of financial sustainability and social impact. This structure is attractive to a growing class of investors and talent and reinforces the project's core values.Primary Revenue Stream: The core business will be a Software-as-a-Service (SaaS) subscription model. Tiered subscription plans will be offered to government departments, public transport operators, and urban planning consultancies. Tiers could be based on the number of users, the scale of the geographical area being analysed, or access to advanced simulation modules. This model provides a predictable, recurring revenue stream necessary for long-term stability.Secondary Revenue Stream: To complement the SaaS offering, the company will provide specialised consulting and training services. This includes helping clients integrate the platform into their workflows, interpret complex simulation outputs, and develop equitable transport policies based on the platform's insights. This not only generates additional revenue but also deepens client relationships and ensures the technology is used to its full potential.Social Mission: The company's articles of incorporation will include a "social lock," mandating that a specified percentage of annual profits be reinvested into further R&D on transport equity and into a community fund that supports advocacy groups working in this area. This formalises the commitment to social impact beyond simply selling a product.Section 6: Implementation Framework: Timeline, Team, and BudgetA credible technology transfer plan must be underpinned by a detailed operational framework that provides the ITC with confidence in the team's ability to execute the proposed strategy.
