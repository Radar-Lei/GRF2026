The research will pioneer the development of multilingual social media analysis technology, customized large-language models (LLMs) for sentiment analysis of gendered travel experiences, and LLM-assisted focus group research. These technological innovations are unique intellectual property assets that follow the context of the Innovation and Technology Agency (ITF) funding program and are commercialized in phases. Its core innovation is to build an integrated framework that transforms qualitative social insights into quantitative and predictable tools for urban planners and transportation operators, thereby opening up new solutions for equitable urban development.

A three-phase funding strategy is proposed to align with specific ITF scheme objectives. Phase One will focus on developing the Minimum Viable Product through the Innovation and Technology Fund for Better Living (FBL), which specifically improve daily living, traveling experiences, and address the needs of specific community groups. This initial phase will develop the core data fusion engine and AI-powered simulation models, leveraging the FBL's focus on social benefit.

Phase Two will transition to the Innovation and Technology Fund Public Sector Trial Scheme (PSTS-ITF), using the prototype system verified in the first phase to conduct field tests with public sector partners such as the Transport Department and MTR Corporation. This stage can be called a bridge between laboratory research and development and practical application. It can not only obtain third-party verification of the practicality of the technology, but also accumulate convincing case models for future commercialization.

The intellectual property generated through this research, including the proprietary algorithms for data fusion, demographic inference, and sentiment analysis of gendered mobility experiences, will be managed through the university's Technology Transfer Office at Phase Three. A formal licensing agreement will be executed with the research group, ensuring appropriate technology transfer while providing a fair return to the university. The business model will center on a Software-as-a-Service subscription platform for government departments and transit operators, complemented by specialized consulting services for policy integration and training. This technology transfer plan aligns with Hong Kong's Smart City Blueprint and the government's priorities in data-driven governance and social innovation. By addressing the globally relevant challenge of transport equity through cutting-edge technology, the project positions Hong Kong as a leader in equitable smart city development while creating economic value.
