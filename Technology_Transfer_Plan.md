
The proposed research will develop innovative methodologies including multilingual social media analytics, customized large language models (LLMs) for sentiment analysis of gendered mobility experiences, and LLM-assisted focus group studies. These technological innovations represent significant intellectual property assets that can be commercialized through a phased approach aligned with Innovation and Technology Fund (ITF) funding schemes. The core innovation lies in the development of a comprehensive framework that transforms qualitative social insights into quantitative, predictive tools for urban planners and transit operators, creating a new category of solutions for equitable urban development.

A three-phase funding strategy is proposed to systematically de-risk the technology development and align with specific ITF scheme objectives. Phase One will focus on developing the Minimum Viable Product through the Innovation and Technology Fund for Better Living (FBL), which supports projects that make daily living safer and address the needs of specific community groups. This initial phase will develop the core data fusion engine and AI-powered simulation models, leveraging the FBL's focus on social benefit and its generous funding terms that support non-profit-making innovation projects.

Phase Two will transition to the Public Sector Trial Scheme for ITF (PSTS-ITF) Projects, utilizing the validated prototype from Phase One to conduct real-world trials with public sector partners such as the Transport Department and MTR Corporation. This phase serves as the bridge between laboratory development and practical implementation, providing third-party validation of the technology's utility and generating compelling case studies for future commercialisation. The PSTS-ITF is specifically designed to facilitate the commercialisation of ITF-funded R&D by supporting prototype production and public sector trials.

Phase Three will culminate in the formation of a university spin-off social enterprise through the Technology Start-up Support Scheme for Universities (TSSSU). With a prototype developed under FBL funding and validated through PSTS trials, the project will be perfectly positioned for commercial spin-off. The TSSSU provides the ideal vehicle to transition the research outcomes into a sustainable social enterprise, offering seed funding for start-up operations and the potential for matching funds through the TSSSU+ component once private investment is secured.

The intellectual property generated through this research, including the proprietary algorithms for data fusion, demographic inference, and sentiment analysis of gendered mobility experiences, will be managed through the university's Technology Transfer Office. A formal licensing agreement will be executed with the spin-off company, ensuring appropriate technology transfer while providing a fair return to the university. The business model will center on a Software-as-a-Service subscription platform for government departments and transit operators, complemented by specialized consulting services for policy integration and training.

This technology transfer plan aligns strategically with Hong Kong's Smart City Blueprint and the government's priorities in data-driven governance and social innovation. By addressing the globally relevant challenge of transport equity through cutting-edge technology, the project positions Hong Kong as a leader in equitable smart city development while creating economic value through the establishment of a new social enterprise. The phased approach demonstrates a sophisticated understanding of the ITF ecosystem and provides a credible roadmap for maximizing the impact of public research investment, ensuring that fundamental discoveries translate into practical solutions that benefit the people of Hong Kong and establish new global standards for inclusive urban mobility.
